Dwarf Fortress is a complex construction, management, and roguelike simulation built around the creation of a fully simulated world rather than simple generated terrain. The game generates civilizations, personalities, creatures, cultures, weather systems, and geology, then places the player in charge of building and sustaining a dwarf fortress within that world. Each generated world presents its own conditions and challenges, including threats such as starvation, dragons, madness, hostile civilizations, wilderness dangers, the dead, and environmental hazards like aquifers or magma mishaps.

At its core, the game tasks players with commanding dwarves as they search for wealth in a generated mountain, while also managing food and beer supplies, construction, and the overall happiness of the colony. Players must read their dwarves’ thoughts, respond to their needs, and try to guide the fortress through inevitable crises. The simulation includes a detailed combat model with skills, body parts, material properties, aimed attacks, wrestling, pain, nausea, poison effects, and other factors. The world also features hundreds of animals and monsters, many of which are randomly generated for each world, along with generated poetry, musical forms, instruments, and dances that dwarves can practice and perform.

The fortress-building side includes crafting treasures and furniture from a variety of materials, establishing a barony, and supporting increasingly demanding nobility. Players can build floodgates to control water for farming or to prevent disasters, and can develop structures and institutions such as taverns, libraries, temples, animal training, bookbinding, and more. Over time, dwarves may discover and build their own culture within the fortress.

The game also includes a dynamic weather model that tracks wind, humidity, and air masses to form fronts, clouds, storms, and blizzards. Its geological simulation can produce over two hundred rock and mineral types in appropriate environments. The world is intended to feel alive and deeply interconnected, with civilization rise and decline emerging from the simulation itself.

The Steam edition adds graphics support, default music, and in-game tutorials to help new players learn the basics, including how to build a first fortress and keep dwarves happy. It features updated pixel art and a 15-track soundtrack with music by Dabu, Simon Swerwer, and Águeda Macias, including tracks in the Dwarvish language. According to the store page, nothing substantial has changed under the hood: it remains the same long-running project, updated since 2003 by Tarn “Toady One” and Zach “Threetoe” Adams, and still aimed at simulating all of existence.
