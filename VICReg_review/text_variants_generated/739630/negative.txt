Phasmophobia presents itself as a 4-player online co-op psychological horror game, but its appeal is built almost entirely on predictable haunted-location routines rather than anything especially fresh or refined. You and up to three other paranormal investigators are dropped into ghost-filled sites, where the main objective is simply to gather evidence, identify the entity, and sell that information to a ghost removal team. In practice, the game leans heavily on repetition: enter a location, collect clues, survive the encounter, and do it again.

The atmosphere is clearly meant to be the main attraction, with realistic graphics and sounds, plus a minimal interface designed to keep players “immersed.” That immersion, however, mostly serves to heighten tension rather than create meaningful variety. The game features over 20 ghost types, each with supposedly unique traits, personalities, and abilities, but the structure still revolves around the same investigation loop, so the differences can feel more like adjustments to the same formula than truly distinct experiences.

Players rely on familiar ghost-hunting tools such as EMF Readers, Spirit Boxes, Thermometers, Night Vision Cameras, and other equipment to uncover paranormal evidence. There are also cursed possessions that can provide information or abilities, but they come at the cost of sanity, reinforcing the game’s punishing, stress-heavy design. Full voice recognition is another gimmick the game depends on, since the ghosts can listen to actual player voices through Ouija Boards, EVP Sessions, and the Spirit Box. While that sounds interesting on paper, it mainly exists to make every interaction feel more precarious.

Phasmophobia offers more than 10 haunted locations, each with different layouts, hiding spots, and twists, along with 5 default difficulties and weekly challenges. There is also custom difficulty, allowing players to tailor games and rewards to their preference, but this flexibility does not change the underlying structure much. The game supports teamwork in a fairly straightforward way: players can investigate directly or sit in the truck monitoring CCTV and motion sensors. That split between active danger and passive support is functional, though hardly innovative.

Ultimately, Phasmophobia is a co-op horror game that depends on atmosphere, voice interaction, and teamwork to carry a formula that can feel narrow and grind-like over time. It may work for groups looking for shared scares, but its reliance on repeated evidence-gathering and survival routines makes it feel more like a tense checklist than a truly remarkable horror experience.
