Metal Unit tries to present itself as a tragic sci-fi struggle for humanity’s survival, but what it really advertises is a fairly standard action roguelite wrapped in familiar apocalyptic dressing. The Earth is supposedly under attack from underground ancient beings and invading alien machines, yet the story mainly exists as a backdrop for controlling Joanna, a new soldier who can pilot the M-Unit suit. Her mission is to save humanity and get revenge on her traitorous sister, who has joined the alien race. It is a melodramatic premise, but one that feels more like functional genre scaffolding than anything genuinely distinctive.

The combat is built around dash-based action, weapon chaining, and a mix of melee and ranged attacks. In theory, this gives players flexibility, but it also sounds like another game leaning heavily on combo language and flashy responsiveness to mask repetition. The game promises devastating chains and varied weapons, but the description offers no indication of deeper tactical complexity beyond swapping between whatever gear the game happens to give you.

Exploration is split between the planet’s surface and underground dungeons, with story progression tied to traveling the world and better rewards hidden below ground. That sounds broad, but also predictably risk-reward driven in the most routine way possible: go deeper, face more danger, maybe get something better. The roguelite structure reinforces that loop by turning all equipped items into permanent research points on death. Those points are then used to unlock new items and skills, which means failure is less a dramatic setback than a mechanism for grinding progression. Instead of feeling exciting, it comes across as another system designed to stretch the game’s life through repetition.

Metal Unit also includes Assist Mode and Challenge Mode, which suggests a game uncertain about its own balance. One mode lets players tailor the experience to their tastes, while the other stacks the odds against them for extra rewards. That kind of split often signals a game trying to appeal to everyone without committing to a coherent difficulty identity.

Overall, Metal Unit appears to be a competent but generic action roguelite with a grim story veneer, familiar combat promises, and progression systems that rely heavily on death, adaptation, and unlock grinding. It may offer variety in weapons and environments, but nothing in its description suggests it rises above the crowded formula it clearly depends on.
