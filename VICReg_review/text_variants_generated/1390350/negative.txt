Webbed presents itself as a small adventure about a happy little spider trying to save her boyfriend from a big mean bowerbird, but the premise is about the only part that sounds remotely bold. The game drops you into a dangerous wilderness and expects you to get by with webs and wits, which means the core of the experience is built around web swinging and using webs to navigate the world faster. In theory, you can leave your own mark by spinning webs across the interconnected environment, sticking them to almost anything and using them to create your own paths.

That freedom sounds pleasant on paper, but the description also makes it clear that Webbed leans heavily on a very specific gimmick rather than offering much beyond it. The spider can make friends with bugs and dance with them, and some of those bugs may help with the rescue effort if you assist them first. Even so, the whole setup feels narrow, with the game relying on basic traversal, light interaction, and a cute rescue premise to carry what seems like a fairly thin adventure.
