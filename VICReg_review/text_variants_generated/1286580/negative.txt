*Ship of Fools* is a co-op roguelite that leans heavily on chaotic naval combat, but not necessarily in a flattering way. You play as one of the “Fools,” absurdly brave sea-farers tasked with defending the Archipelago after the Great Lighthouse has been broken and an “Aquapocalypse” rolls in. The setup sounds dramatic, yet the actual premise is straightforward: board The Stormstrider, fire cannons at sea monsters, repair your ship, and repeat the process across multiple runs.

The game is built around two-player co-op, though solo play is also available. In theory, teamwork matters because you must man the ship, protect it from attacks, and combine item effects for stronger outcomes. In practice, that probably means a lot of frantic scrambling while you try not to get turned into fish food. The game also includes local multiplayer, with a controller required for that mode.

Progression comes from unlocking trinkets and artifacts, with over 100 items to collect and combine into builds. You can also encounter islands, lost treasures, and remote shops while navigating the Archipelago. The catch is that the Everlasting Storm constantly shifts the map, blocking paths and forcing you to adjust your route. That adds some tension, but it also sounds like the kind of structure that exists mainly to keep you second-guessing every decision.

Ship of Fools includes both solo and multiplayer runs, several unique Fools with special abilities, and colossal leviathans as major threats. Its hand-drawn artwork is one of the few clearly appealing parts of the package. Overall, it presents itself as a fast, co-op-focused roguelite about maritime defense, but everything about it seems designed to funnel players into repeated, chaotic encounters rather than anything especially elegant or reliable.
