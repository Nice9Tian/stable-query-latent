*Let's Build a Zoo* is a management sim that tries very hard to sound charming while piling on systems that mostly amount to busywork. The pitch is straightforward: build your own zoo, import and breed animals, hire staff, keep visitors satisfied, and handle a steady stream of odd events. It also adds DNA splicing as a gimmick, letting you combine creatures into absurd hybrids like a Giraffephant or a PandOwl. In total, the game claims over 500 animals and more than 300,000 possible combinations, which sounds impressive until you realize it is mostly a numbers game.

The core loop revolves around expanding an animal park with hundreds of buildings, foliage, paths, and enclosure decorations. You are expected to manage everything from what your animals eat to what eats your animals, which suggests the simulation leans more toward micromanagement than meaningful creativity. Staff hiring is another layer, as you must pick the “right” employees to handle the game’s various life events. That is all wrapped in the usual visitor-happiness routine, a familiar management obligation that can easily feel repetitive.

The game also offers choices about legality, allowing you to run the zoo properly or break the law for extra money. That sounds like a sharp idea on paper, but in practice it is just another management branch in a game already trying to juggle too much. Likewise, importing animals from other zoos, breeding them into families, or manufacturing them through DNA splicing all feed into the same accumulation-driven structure.

In the end, *Let's Build a Zoo* is less a focused zoo builder than a sprawling collection of features competing for attention. It advertises variety, but much of that variety is just different ways to maintain the same park, chase profits, and deal with increasingly silly animal mashups. If the goal was to make zoo management feel wild, the result seems more cluttered than exciting.
