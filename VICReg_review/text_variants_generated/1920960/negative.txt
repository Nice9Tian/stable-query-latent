VPet-Simulator presents itself as a free desktop pet software game, but the page makes it clear that the experience is mostly a promotional side project for VUP Simulator rather than a standout standalone product. It is open source, supports Steam Workshop, and allows users to interact with a virtual pet through a wide range of actions and animations. The store page boasts more than 200 types of animation, with examples such as touching the head or body, bringing up dance, and climbing the wall.

In theory, the feature list sounds expansive. In practice, the game’s own description leans heavily on external tools and community content to carry it. The Workshop support is framed as a major selling point, letting players add or modify table pet animations, items, food, beverages, speech text, themes, and even code plugins. It also claims that users can extend the desktop pet with new animation logic, display schemes like l2d or spin, and additional functions such as alarms or dashboards. That said, the page’s “almost omnipotent” claim feels more like hype than a grounded promise.

The game is completely free and open source on GitHub, and players are invited to contribute ideas, participate in development, or modify the code to make their own table pet. This openness is commendable, but it also suggests that the core product may be more of a framework than a fully polished game. The store text repeatedly emphasizes that most content already supports the Workshop, reducing the need for code changes, which again reinforces how dependent the experience is on community expansion.

Overall, VPet-Simulator appears to be a basic desktop pet platform with lots of advertised interactivity, but its presentation is disjointed and overly promotional. It is not really trying to hide that its main purpose is to push interest toward VUP Simulator. For users looking for a simple, free, customizable pet toy, it may have some appeal, but the page offers little reason to expect anything deeper than a modest open-source desktop companion with a large amount of user-supplied content.
