Demonologist is a co-op horror game for 1 to 4 players, built around entering cursed locations, identifying the type of evil spirit present, and attempting to exorcise it using your equipment. On paper, that sounds like a standard paranormal investigation loop, and the page does little to suggest anything deeper than the usual routine of gathering clues, reacting to random ghost events, and repeating the same process in different places. The game also claims to offer “the best graphics in the co-op horror genre,” but this kind of self-congratulatory marketing does not tell you much beyond the fact that it was developed in Unreal Engine 5.

The actual structure seems heavily reliant on familiar horror-game systems: random events, ghosts appearing in each session, and the need to be careful with your speech because cursed places react to what you say. The idea that a single word can open a secret door is at least an interesting gimmick, but it also sounds like the sort of feature that could easily become arbitrary and frustrating rather than genuinely clever. The game allows you to invite other players to a safe house, choose paranormal locations to play in, and display achievements in a showcase, which gives the impression of a fairly standard hub setup with cosmetic progression layered on top.

Progression appears to revolve around earning money to upgrade your safe house, buy a new one, or purchase a pet to stand with you there. None of this sounds especially meaningful to the core horror experience; it mostly reads like checklist content meant to pad out the loop. The game also includes multiple maps and equipment options, daily and weekly tasks, and special-date events and missions, which suggests a serviceable amount of content but not necessarily a distinctive one. Overall, Demonologist seems to lean on cooperative ghost hunting and repeated task-based progression, while relying on atmosphere and marketing claims to cover for a very conventional design.
