*The Quarry* is a teen-horror game that tries to sell itself as a cinematic thriller, but beneath the polished presentation it is built on a familiar, heavily guided structure. The story begins on the last day of summer camp at Hackett’s Quarry, where the teenage counselors celebrate with a party that quickly collapses into chaos. What starts as carefree banter and flirtation turns into a night of blood, fear, and forced decisions, as the counselors are hunted by violent locals and something even worse lurking beneath the surface.

The core structure is choice-driven. You play as all nine camp counselors across a branching narrative where every decision affects who survives and how events unfold. The game repeatedly frames this as a tense web of possibilities, but the premise is still rooted in deciding whether to investigate danger, flee, or try to save others while the situation spirals out of control. Any character can become central to the story—or die before daylight arrives. That is the game’s main hook, but also its bleakest limitation: survival feels less like mastery and more like a parade of avoidable disasters.

The cinematic presentation is clearly a major selling point. The game uses facial capture, filmic lighting, and a cast of Hollywood actors to create what it presents as a horror movie you actively steer. The cast includes David Arquette, Ariel Winter, Brenda Song, Evan Evagora, Halston Sage, Justice Smith, Miles Robbins, Siobhan Williams, Skyler Gisondo, Zach Tinker, Ethan Suplee, Grace Zabriskie, Lance Henriksen, Lin Shaye, and Ted Raimi. The result is meant to feel like an ensemble horror film, though the store page’s emphasis on production values suggests that presentation is doing a lot of the heavy lifting.

There are also several ways the game is packaged and styled. The Deluxe Edition adds a bonus content pack with ‘80s-themed character outfits, instant access to the Death Rewind system, the Gorefest Movie Mode option, and a Horror History Visual Filter Pack. That filter pack offers three visual styles: an 8mm-inspired grainy indie horror look, a retro VHS-style ‘80s horror filter, and a classic black-and-white cinematic filter. These additions reinforce the game’s obsession with horror aesthetics, but they also make it sound more like a curated theme park of genre references than a particularly original horror experience.

Multiplayer is another part of the pitch. Up to seven friends can join online to watch, vote on key decisions, and influence the story through Wolf Pack Mode, while couch co-op lets each player control a counselor. There is also an adjustable difficulty system for all gameplay elements, and Movie Mode allows the entire game to be watched rather than played, as if the developers expected some players to prefer passive consumption over direct involvement. The Official Trial Version even lets players experience the Prologue for free, with story progress carrying over if they later buy the full game on the same Steam account.

Overall, *The Quarry* is designed around horror atmosphere, branching choices, and cinematic presentation, but it leans so hard on those features that the experience can feel more calculated than compelling. It offers teenage survival drama, multiplayer voting, visual filters, and a bundle of edition extras, yet the store page’s constant emphasis on style and options makes it seem desperate to mask how conventional the underlying formula really is.
