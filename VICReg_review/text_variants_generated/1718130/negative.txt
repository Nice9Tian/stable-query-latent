*SCP: Secret Files* is a disappointing attempt to turn the SCP Foundation into an anthology-style game, but it never escapes the feeling of being a showcase for ideas rather than a coherent, compelling experience. The premise is straightforward enough: rookie researcher Karl is assigned to organize top-secret Foundation files, only to have his perception of reality distorted as he browses the archives. From there, the game leans on the familiar SCP setup of humanity facing terrifying anomalies, with the Foundation presented as the secretive organization meant to “secure, contain, and protect.” That material is undeniably rich, but the game seems content to rest on the strength of the source lore instead of building something equally memorable.

The game advertises a variety of distinct scenarios, and that variety is one of its few notable points: investigating a mysterious hydroelectric power plant, moving through an “uplifting fable,” and exploring a bizarre retro-inspired world. Each story is said to bring its own tone, mechanics, and cast of characters. In practice, this reads more like a collection of disconnected experiments than a unified experience, which may appeal to SCP fans looking for scattered curiosities, but it also suggests a lack of focus. The promise of different gameplay styles does not automatically make for satisfying design, especially when the page gives no reason to believe these ideas are developed with much depth.

The game also positions itself as both an SCP fan’s dream and a friendly entry point for newcomers, since it covers a broad range of SCP material: people, creatures, objects, phenomena, abstract concepts, and even supreme beings like SCP-3999. Its themes are equally wide-ranging, spanning horror, science fiction, comedy, mythology, and more. Yet this breadth can feel more like an excuse to throw everything at the wall than a sign of strong direction. Instead of offering a focused identity, *SCP: Secret Files* seems to rely on the assumption that SCP’s reputation alone will carry the experience.

Overall, the page describes a game with an appealing premise and ambitious scope, but the result sounds uneven and overdependent on borrowed mythology. It may be interesting for those already invested in SCP lore, but as presented, it comes across as a fragmented anthology that promises strange encounters without convincing you that the whole package will be worth the time.
