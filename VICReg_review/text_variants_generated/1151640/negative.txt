Horizon Zero Dawn™ Complete Edition is a visually ambitious action role-playing game, but its appeal is heavily dependent on whether one can tolerate its familiar open-world structure and relentless emphasis on combat checks. The game follows Aloy, an outcast hunter trying to uncover her past, learn her destiny, and prevent a looming catastrophe in a world overtaken by deadly Machines. That premise has some intrigue, but the store-page framing leans hard on grand mystery without showing much that feels truly original in execution.

Gameplay centers on tactical attacks against unique Machines and rival tribes while exploring an open world filled with wildlife and danger. In practice, that means the game is built around the usual loop of roaming, fighting, gathering, and pushing toward the next story thread. The emphasis on “devastating” and “tactical” combat suggests a system that wants to feel strategic, yet the page offers little evidence that it rises above standard action-RPG design. It is clearly a game about confronting enemies repeatedly rather than offering especially varied interaction with its world.

The Complete Edition includes The Frozen Wilds expansion, which adds new lands, skills, weapons, and Machines. It also bundles several outfits, bows, packs, and a digital art book. While that content list makes the package appear generous, it also reads like a straightforward pile of extras rather than something that meaningfully transforms the core experience. The additions seem aimed more at extending the same formula than addressing any underlying weaknesses.

As a PC release, this Complete Edition is presented as the full version of a multi-award-winning title, but the store text mostly sells scale, danger, and lore instead of convincing depth. For players already interested in a machine-hunting open world, it may be enough. For everyone else, it looks like another large, polished, and mechanically familiar adventure that struggles to justify its reputation through the page alone.
