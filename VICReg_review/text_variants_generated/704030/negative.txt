Professional Farmer: Cattle and Crops tries to sell itself as a new generation farming simulator, but the result reads more like a checklist of ambition than a truly convincing experience. It promises depth, detail, and realism across nearly every part of farm life: field work, livestock care, harvesting, and the full cycle of food production. On paper, it covers a lot, yet the presentation leans heavily on its features rather than on any sense of genuine excitement.

The game focuses on detailed machinery and practical farm operations. Players can work their fields with original machines or assign workers to handle tasks instead. It includes sowing, fertilizing, harvesting, grassland work such as mowing, turning, windrowing, and baling, plus physical bale handling with collecting and stacking square bales using a front loader. Vehicle control is described as convenient and flexible, using a radial menu and quickbar, while support for steering wheels and controllers is also included. Realistic vehicle physics and multiple machine functions are clearly central to the experience.

Its farming systems are also built around supposed authenticity, though this emphasis can feel overbearing. Plant growth depends on proper fertilization with nutrients like nitrogen, phosphate, and potassium, and weather is simulated using real weather data that affects soil and crop growth. Fields are dynamic too, with machinery creating lanes and tracks that remain even after saving. The game also features livestock management and a detailed machinery system, but the overall pitch is dominated by realism for realism’s sake.

Professional Farmer: Cattle and Crops adds a management layer through an intelligent helper system and a calendar-based planning structure. Employees can autonomously control machines across fields and roads, and they can be organized for cooperative tasks. This sounds practical, but it also suggests a game that wants to automate much of itself while still demanding constant oversight. Tutorials and scenarios are included to introduce agriculture and provide background knowledge, which further implies that the game expects players to learn a lot before they can do much at all.

The structure includes career mode, tutorials, scenarios, and sandbox mode, along with experience levels, XP, reputation, and achievements. Difficulty ranges from arcade gamer to simulation professional, which at least gives players some control over how punishing the game becomes. Mod support is also available for machines and maps, letting players add their own content or use creations from others. The developers say they worked closely with Landwirtschaftsverlag Münster to make the simulation as realistic as possible, but the result still comes across as more technical than engaging.

In the end, Professional Farmer: Cattle and Crops seems overloaded with systems meant to signal authenticity, yet it risks turning farming into a rigid exercise in management and maintenance. It may offer detailed machinery, realistic weather, and extensive planning tools, but its obsession with simulation depth may be exactly what makes it feel less like a rewarding farm life experience and more like a laborious chore.
