**Out of Space** tries to sell itself as a quirky co-op survival strategy game, but its own premise is basically a laundry list of chores in a floating house. You and your friends move into a procedurally generated spaceship home, then spend your time dealing with alien infestation, trash, goo, power management, food production, and room expansion. The pitch is all about “living together,” but what that really means here is constantly cleaning, recycling, and organizing a cramped mess before it gets worse.

The game supports local and online multiplayer, plus single-player, and it leans heavily on short, easy-to-start sessions as a couch-friendly experience. At the same time, it wants to be a strategy game too, forcing players to make short- and long-term decisions about how to expand rooms, automate tasks, and keep everything sustainable. In practice, that means buying, building, and placing technologies, recycling alien goo into energy batteries, harvesting gardens, and trying to keep everyone fed and alive. There are also robots for automation and dogs you can adopt to help out, though even these additions sound more like minor comforts than meaningful solutions.

Out of Space also makes a point of saying there are no weapons at all, which fits its “mop your way out of danger” approach. Instead of combat, the game revolves around cleaning rooms with mops and water while containing the alien threat. Missions guide progression, achievements unlock new content, and each run is supposed to be different thanks to procedural generation. Still, the core loop sounds repetitive: clean, recycle, expand, automate, repeat.

The result is a game that seems more interested in turning domestic inconvenience into a management problem than in offering anything genuinely exciting. Even the description of taking a nap on the sofa while friends desperately ask for help makes the whole thing feel more like a stressful housekeeping simulator than a fun space adventure. Developed by Behold Studios, it promises community feedback and ongoing improvement, but from the page alone, Out of Space comes across as a cluttered co-op obligation dressed up as a charming party game.
