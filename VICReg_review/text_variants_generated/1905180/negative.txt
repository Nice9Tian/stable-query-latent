OBS Studio is a free, open-source streaming and recording tool that does what it advertises, but not much more than that. It is built for livestreaming and local video capture, letting users create scenes from a mix of sources such as display capture, games, webcams, video files, browser windows, images, text, and capture cards. In practice, it is less a polished creative suite than a dense utility packed with configuration options.

The software supports streaming to Twitch, YouTube, Facebook Live, and other services, including custom servers, while also allowing recordings to be saved locally. It offers real-time video and audio capturing and mixing, a multi-source scene system, unlimited scene switching with custom transitions, and an audio mixer with filters like noise gate, noise suppression, gain, and VST plugin support. There are also hotkeys for many actions, Studio Mode for previewing scenes before they go live, and Multiview for monitoring up to 8 scenes at once.

OBS Studio also leans heavily into customization, with a modular dock UI, pop-out panels, an extensive Settings panel, and support for duplicated sources and adjustable properties. But all of that flexibility comes with a clunky, technical feel that can be overwhelming, especially for users who just want a straightforward recording or streaming setup. It is powerful and flexible, certainly, but it is also the kind of tool that seems to demand patience before it becomes remotely comfortable to use.
