PARANORMASIGHT: The Seven Mysteries of Honjo tries to sell itself as an elaborate horror-adventure visual novel, but what it really offers is a gloomy tangle of curses, investigations, and fatal coincidences set in late-20th-century Sumida, Tokyo. The premise leans heavily on the so-called “Seven Mysteries of Honjo” and a mysterious Rite of Resurrection, yet the game seems more interested in piling on supernatural conflict than in delivering a sharply grounded mystery.

You follow Shogo Okiie, an ordinary office worker whose late-night trip to Kinshibori Park with his friend Yoko Fukunaga drags him into a series of strange events tied to local ghost stories. He is joined by other characters with their own grim motives: detectives probing a series of suspicious deaths, a high-school girl chasing the truth behind a classmate’s suicide, and a mother bent on revenge for her lost son. Their stories intersect around curses and resurrection, but the setup feels less like elegant intrigue and more like a dense web of misery.

Gameplay is described as investigation-driven, with conversations, clue gathering, and movement choices that can lead to death if handled carelessly. The game also claims strategic curse management, where timing your own curses matters or else you risk being cursed in return. That sounds more like a narrow decision system than a truly dynamic horror experience. The visual presentation uses 360° photo-sphere backgrounds recreated from real locations in Sumida, which may aim for atmosphere, but it also suggests the game is relying heavily on ambience to carry what is otherwise a heavily exposition-driven story.

In the end, PARANORMASIGHT appears to be a text-heavy supernatural thriller that depends on twisting point-of-view storytelling and a battle of curses rather than on particularly inventive interactivity. It is built around mystery, but its appeal seems limited to players willing to endure a bleak, often melodramatic narrative in exchange for a few strategic choices and some carefully reconstructed scenery.
