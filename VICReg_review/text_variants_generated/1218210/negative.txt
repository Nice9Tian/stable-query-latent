Coromon presents itself as a classic monster-catching RPG, but what it really offers is a fairly familiar formula wrapped in a modern pixel-art coat. You play as a newly appointed Battle Researcher for Lux Solis on the world of Velua, only for your first day to collapse into an attack by a mysterious force. From there, the game sends you off to assemble a squad of Coromon, hunt down the invaders, and confront a larger alien threat that gradually unfolds through a story meant to feel grand, though it remains firmly within well-worn JRPG territory.

The core gameplay revolves around taming and battling more than 100 Coromon, each defined by elements, traits, and skills. You catch them, train them, and then use them against wildlife, trainers, and titanic bosses. The battle system is stamina-based, which means every action costs energy, forcing players to pay attention to resource management rather than simply attacking mindlessly. Status effects, stamina damage, and a range of conditions can be used to weaken opponents or disable them entirely. It is a system built around careful choice, but it also makes combat feel more methodical than exciting.

Exploration covers six major zones and cities connected by routes filled with quests, secrets, and items. The environments range from frozen glacial caverns to blistering deserts, which at least gives the map some variety, even if the structure is clearly that of a conventional RPG journey. The narrative promises twists and revelations, but the store text itself makes it sound more like a standard “something bigger is going on” setup than a truly original plot.

Coromon also leans heavily on customization and difficulty options, perhaps to compensate for the familiar design. Players can adjust difficulty in ways that alter fundamental mechanics, not just enemy stats, and there are built-in Randomizer and Nuzlocke modes for those who want extra challenge. You can also customize your trainer’s appearance with hundreds of options, and even change Coromon colors through the latest update. The Lux Kitchen, fruit-to-cake conversion, and new cake recipes are also mentioned, though these additions feel more like decorative extras than meaningful systems.

As a tribute to older creature-collecting RPGs, Coromon clearly wants to honor the classics, but that ambition mostly results in a game that feels safe and derivative. It has a decent amount of content, a recognizable retro aesthetic, and a battle system with some tactical weight, yet it does little to escape the shadow of the games it imitates.
