Evil Genius 2: World Domination tries to revive the cult appeal of its predecessor, but what it offers is mostly a familiar management loop dressed in satirical spy-fi packaging. It is a lair builder where you play as an Evil Genius, construct an island base, train criminal minions, fend off the Forces of Justice, and eventually attempt world domination with a Doomsday Device. The premise sounds gleefully wicked, but the game largely leans on the same basic promise of building, defending, and managing, only with a louder sense of irony.

The core of the game is building a personal evil lair and cover operation. You are told to pick an island paradise and shape its internal structure to suit your play style, then fill it with “wonderfully wicked devices” for your minions to use. In practice, this is standard base construction wrapped in villain branding. The game presents this as “Cruel Constructions,” but it is still just layout management, resource planning, and placing rooms and gadgets in the hope that the whole operation functions.

Minions are another major pillar, yet even here the game frames ordinary workforce management as something sinister and grandiose. You expand and train your minion workforce, then create specialists to support your schemes. If that is not enough, you can recruit Henchmen to act as more powerful right hands. The page emphasizes that competence is not enough for an evil operation, but the underlying system still sounds like a conventional layer of staffing and upgrade progression with an edgy coat of paint.

Defense is handled through trap networks, which are meant to complement your brute force. The examples given include a Pinball Bumper and a Venus Spy-Trap, and the game encourages chaining traps together to remove intruders. This is one of the more clearly defined mechanics on the page, but it still reads as a typical tower-defense-like gimmick embedded in a management game. The promise that “more is evil-er” does not hide the fact that the design is built on repeated trap placement and enemy disruption.

The world domination side is delivered through “Nefarious Plots,” which include absurd objectives like selling the British Royal Family, kidnapping the Governor of Maine, and “literally BAKE ALASKA.” These schemes are meant to be humorous, and the game claims to offer hundreds of potential objectives, but the exaggeration only goes so far. The missions appear to be the main source of variety, yet the page itself suggests a lot of the experience is still about executing one villainous errand after another.

There is also a Season Pass, which promises a new Genius, a Campaign Pack, a Lair Items Pack, two Henchmen Packs, and two Minion Packs. That content may expand the game, but on the store page it mainly signals a structure built around DLC from the outset. The pass is even included in the Deluxe Edition, which reinforces the impression that the base game is being positioned as a platform for added material rather than a complete standalone experience.

Overall, Evil Genius 2: World Domination sells itself on satire, but beneath the jokes it is a fairly standard lair-management game with traps, minion training, and mission objectives. Its villainous tone does the heavy lifting, while the mechanics sound functional but not especially inventive. It may let you feel bad in a cheerful way, but the store page does little to suggest that the game rises above its basic formula.
