*Banished* is a city-building strategy game built around a harsh, unforgiving cycle of survival rather than comfort or progress. You lead a group of exiled travelers who must begin again in a new land with only the clothes on their backs and a cart of supplies. The people themselves are the core resource, and the game makes that clear in a relentlessly practical way: they are born, age, work, raise children, and eventually die. Keeping them healthy, happy, and fed is not optional—it is the entire basis for whether a settlement can function at all.

The game strips away many common city-building systems. There are no skill trees, and no money system. Any structure can be built at any time as long as the required resources have been gathered, which sounds flexible but really just shifts the burden onto careful resource management. Trade is handled through merchants and trade vessels, which can be used to barter for goods. These traders are also one of the few ways to bring livestock and annual crops into the town’s diet, but their long trade routes also create a risk of importing diseases, making even basic expansion feel like a liability.

Occupations cover a wide range, including farming, hunting, blacksmithing, mining, teaching, and healing, with twenty different jobs available in total. Yet the game offers no easy or universal solution. Success depends heavily on the specific map, since resources may be more limited in one location than another. Players can replant forests, mine iron, and quarry rock, but every one of those choices consumes space that can no longer be used for expansion. That means progress constantly collides with the limits of the land itself.

Rather than encouraging creativity or growth, *Banished* appears to revolve around brittle survival and constant compromise. Towns rise or collapse based on how well risks and resources are managed, and the game’s systems seem designed to punish mistakes rather than reward ambition.
