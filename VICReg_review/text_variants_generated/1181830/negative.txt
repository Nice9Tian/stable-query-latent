*Urtuk: The Desolation* is presented as an open-world tactical turn-based RPG, but what it offers sounds less like an inviting adventure and more like a bleak grind through ruin and suffering. Set in a low-fantasy dark world, it sends players to guide a band of adventurers across the remnants of an ancient civilization, scavenging loot from corpses and struggling to survive in a hostile, unforgiving landscape.

The game leans heavily into survival and combat rather than any sense of wonder. Its turn-based battles are described as tactically rich, played out on large maps with environmental factors shaping every encounter. Characters can be pushed into spikes or off cliffs, shielded allies can counterattack, archers can provide ranged support, and combo attacks are part of the system. The emphasis is clearly on using terrain and positioning, but the overall tone remains grim and brutal.

Customization revolves around extracting skills and traits directly from fallen enemies and applying them to your own units. These extracted abilities can significantly alter a character’s role, adding effects such as pushing enemies on critical hits, retaliating when struck, avoiding lethal blows, or landing lifestealing counterattacks. Characters also unlock new skills by performing specific actions in battle, which means the game rewards repeated tactical experimentation, though that structure may feel more like a checklist than genuine progression.

The campaign itself is procedurally generated, reinforcing the sense of instability and repetition. The game also features hand-drawn graphical assets across characters, monsters, objects, the world map, and battlefields, which may be one of its few clearly distinctive qualities. Even so, the presentation appears to serve a world defined by misery rather than discovery.

The story is equally joyless. This is a world without elves, dwarves, dragons, or the usual fantasy comforts. Centuries ago, Giants lived peacefully alongside humans until war wiped them out. Later, scientists discovered a way to extract the Life Essence from Giant bones, hoping it could cure disease, heal wounds, or even slow aging. Instead, it often causes mutations or fatal illness. Despite that, experimentation continues on unwilling subjects.

The protagonist, Urtuk, escapes from a Sanatorium where abducted commoners are used in these experiments. After suffering a severe mutation from exposure to the Life Essence, he steadily deteriorates and must search for a cure before his condition kills him. That premise sets the tone for the entire game: a harsh, decaying world where survival is precarious, hope is thin, and even the central character is running out of time.
