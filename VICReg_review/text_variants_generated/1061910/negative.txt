Metal: Hellsinger is an overstyled rhythm FPS that leans hard on its premise because the rest of it is mostly just a familiar gauntlet of demons, arenas, and upgrade choices. You play as The Unknown, a part-human, part-demon figure bent on vengeance, fighting through the Infernal Planes to confront the Red Judge. The setting tries to sound grand by presenting Hell as “a union of a thousand Hells,” but it still boils down to marching through hostile zones like the icy world of Voke and the maddening world of Stygia.

The core gimmick is shooting on the beat. If you keep in sync with the rhythm, your gameplay supposedly improves, the music intensifies, and your damage output rises. In theory, this creates a tighter bond between combat and soundtrack; in practice, it is just another system asking you to perform correctly before the game really rewards you for it. You can use a skull-clad blade or a range of weapons to fight demon hordes, and once your ultimate is charged, you can unleash a chosen weapon ability. There is also the Leviathan mode, where increasingly difficult waves of demons attack you in hellish arenas. Progress is tied to making upgrade decisions and unlocking special challenges, apparently so you can climb the leaderboards and become more powerful.

Music is one of the main selling points, but even that is built around a very explicit commercial pitch. Every track was created specifically for the game by Two Feathers, with vocals from metal artists including Serj Tankian, Randy Blythe, and Alissa White-Gluz. The campaign is narrated by Troy Baker, which is meant to add weight to an “epic storyline” that the page clearly wants you to care about. Metal: Hellsinger comes from The Outsiders and is described as a passion project from David Goldfarb, but the page mostly reads like a checklist of genre hooks: rhythm-based shooting, demon-slaying, challenge modes, leaderboards, celebrity voices, and infernal lore. It sounds aggressive, polished, and loud, but not especially original.
