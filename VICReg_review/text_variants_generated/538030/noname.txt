This strategy game places the player in command of a multinational military organization fighting an extraterrestrial threat from the shadows. Set in an alternate 2009 where the Cold War continues and the Berlin Wall still stands, the world is already strained by political tension when mysterious sightings, disappearances, and escalating panic reveal that the alien presence is growing bolder. The player must operate beyond national boundaries, gathering intelligence, responding to incursions, and building a covert global network capable of confronting an enemy that is both advanced and elusive.

A central part of the experience is managing multiple hidden bases around the world. Each base can be designed and specialized for different roles, such as detection, rapid response, research, manufacturing, or defense. The construction system uses a grid layout, and placement matters because fuel range and regional coverage affect how efficiently forces can be deployed. Bases can house fighter squadrons, transport aircraft, radar systems, research teams, operatives, and other support structures, making strategic expansion a major part of progression.

Combat unfolds on two fronts: tactical ground missions and real-time air engagements. On the ground, players command troops in dangerous battles where positioning, ammunition, line of sight, cover, and terrain all matter. Missions take place across a variety of locations and objectives, and enemies actively maneuver, seek flanking positions, use cover, and take the high ground. The enemy force includes many alien species with different strengths, weaknesses, and combat styles, as well as human agents working in support of the invasion. These human opponents introduce additional mission types, battlefields, and objectives, including infiltration-style operations to uncover their network and seize information.

The game emphasizes learning through combat. Defeated enemies can provide research samples, and securing alien bodies allows for autopsies that reveal weaknesses and improve preparation for future encounters. Captured technology can also be reverse-engineered, feeding into a broad research tree with more than one hundred projects. This research unlocks a wide range of aircraft, vehicles, weapons, armor, automated combat platforms, and sentry guns, allowing players to shape their forces in different ways. Heavy mechanized units can help break through cover and deliver powerful attacks, though their use may reduce the ability to recover valuable materials from intact enemies and equipment.

Air combat is handled as a strategic and tactical layer of its own. Players scramble jets to intercept unidentified craft, direct pilots in real time, and manage fuel, maneuvering, and interception positioning. Rather than relying on one-on-one duels, the system allows multiple aircraft to coordinate against stronger enemy ships. Air operations and ground operations are closely linked, so managing hangar placement, response range, and base coverage becomes essential.

Between missions, soldiers can be trained at bases and improve through actual combat experience. Their abilities develop according to what they do in battle, and they can earn medals for notable actions. Wounds and casualties are common, and survival matters because veterans become valuable assets over time. The game presents war as costly and unforgiving, with no guarantee of success and many losses along the way.

Overall, this is a globally scaled tactical strategy game built around difficult battles, layered base management, research progression, and constant pressure from an intelligent alien invasion. Its systems are designed to interact closely, so every decision in deployment, engineering, and combat can influence the wider campaign. It is also a standalone experience, intended to be approachable without prior familiarity with the earlier entry in the series.
