Untitled Goose Game is built around a deliberately obnoxious premise and mostly commits to it with exhausting consistency: you are a “horrible goose” unleashed on an unsuspecting village, and the entire point is to bother everyone in sight. The game describes itself as a slapstick-stealth-sandbox, but that combination really just means you sneak around town causing petty chaos. You move through back gardens, high street shops, and the village green, setting up pranks, stealing hats, honking constantly, and generally ruining people’s day.

The game’s sense of humor is simple and repetitive, leaning hard on the idea that being disruptive is inherently funny. It offers a dedicated honk button, as if one low-effort joke needed a whole feature callout, and the rest of the experience centers on behaving as annoyingly as possible toward ordinary villagers who are simply trying to get on with their day. If that premise wears thin quickly, the game does little to disguise it.

A later update adds two-player cooperative mode, letting two horrible geese play through the whole game together. This means double the honking, double the pranks, and double the unnecessary disruption. It is a free update for all owners, but it does not fundamentally change the game’s mean-spirited core; it just spreads the nuisance around. In the end, Untitled Goose Game is less a deep stealth adventure than a short, smug exercise in being a menace.
