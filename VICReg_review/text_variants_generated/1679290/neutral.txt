Fire & Maneuver is a turn-based strategy game set in Victorian warfare, developed by the Armchair History team for players interested in history, wargames, and strategy. The game aims to balance historical authenticity with accessibility, combining units based on historical research with mechanics designed to remain engaging and playable.

The game focuses entirely on military victory, placing players in command during a period of rapid tactical and technological change. Players can fight historical battles against friends or explore alternate outcomes by diverging from history. Campaign decisions involve managing offensive pressure carefully, since constant attacks can drain resources and make sustained aggression costly.

A key feature of Fire & Maneuver is its simultaneous turn system. Rather than waiting for an opponent to act and then reacting, players must predict enemy intentions before issuing orders. Available actions include withdrawing to better ground, attacking, or digging in to hold position. Orders are final once issued, so success depends on anticipation and tactical judgment.

The campaign mode includes four campaigns: the Franco-Prussian War, the Crimean War, the Austro-Prussian War, and the Caucasus Theater. Each campaign can be played from either side and also in multiplayer against a friend. The game includes division customization, troop personalization through a regiment skill tree, a historical event system, and a military doctrine system, all of which are intended to make each playthrough different. It also introduces a linear scenario system for fighting through connected historical battles, including recreations of campaigns such as the Expedition of the Thousand.

Multiplayer supports both preset historical scenarios and more open alternate-history matchups. Players can take on the roles of historical commanders such as Moltke or Bazaine, or create unconventional battles such as British redcoats facing German Krupp guns on the steppes of Russia. The game emphasizes rewarding skillful generalship over luck.

Fire & Maneuver features several factions spanning the years 1853 to 1871, with armies developing through early and late periods of play. The Second French Empire offers a balanced force with durable Foreign Legion units but faces numerical and artillery disadvantages. The North German Confederation fields strong commanders and mixed German-state units, including Krupp artillery, though its rifles have short range. The British Empire relies on disciplined infantry and Highlanders, but its units are expensive. The Russian Empire uses mass infantry attacks and Cossack cavalry, while the Ottoman Empire fields large numbers of militiamen and the Bashi Bazouks, though with weak discipline. The Austrian Empire combines two styles of warfare, including strong skirmishers, but can struggle at closer range. The Kingdom of Italy relies on nationalism, mountain troops, and political paramilitaries, though its specialized troops lack professionalism.
