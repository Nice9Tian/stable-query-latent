Holdfast: Nations At War is a largely community-driven multiplayer shooter that leans hard into roleplay, but not always to its benefit. The game presents itself as a first- and third-person experience built around historic warfare on land and sea, yet much of its appeal appears to depend on organised player behavior, proximity voice chat, and regiment participation rather than on any especially compelling core design.

Its battles are pitched as large-scale clashes with over 150 players per server, spanning multiple eras such as the Napoleonic Wars and World War One. In practice, this means charging across open fields, standing in musket lines, firing coordinated volleys, manning cannons, or scrambling through coastal siege scenarios. The game also offers naval combat aboard warships from the age of sail, where players are expected to cooperate to keep vessels afloat and fight as a crew. These ideas sound ambitious, but they are clearly built around spectacle and player coordination more than refined gameplay systems.

The class structure is broad, with 26 classes available and each carrying its own traits and abilities. Players can serve in the army, navy, or coast guard, and can take on roles such as officer, artillery support, or bayonet-wielding infantry. Mounted classes like hussars and dragoons add horseback movement and combat, letting players swing swords or fire pistols and carbines from the saddle. Even so, the page frames most of this as teamwork-dependent functionality, which suggests a game that may be more demanding of coordination than genuinely accessible.

Progression and customization are present, but they seem to be mostly cosmetic. Players can unlock heads, equip weapons from other nations, and even adjust the pitch of their character’s voice for shouts and calls. This does at least give some room for personal expression, though it does not sound like enough to elevate the experience beyond its social and roleplay emphasis.

Faction choice is also central, with allegiance options including the British Empire, French Empire, Kingdom of Prussia, Russian Empire, Kingdom of Italy, Central Powers, and Allied Powers. Alongside this, regiments function as in-game clans, pushing players into organised events and formations that mirror Napoleonic-era warfare. The game clearly wants disciplined teamwork, but that kind of structure can easily turn the experience rigid and exclusionary for players who are not already invested in that style of play.

Ultimately, Holdfast: Nations At War seems less like a polished shooter and more like a sprawling multiplayer stage for organised roleplay, historical reenactment, and communal chaos. Its scale, class variety, naval battles, and voice-driven social features may appeal to dedicated groups, but the overall impression is of a game that relies heavily on its community to keep itself interesting.
