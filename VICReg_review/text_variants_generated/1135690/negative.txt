Unpacking presents itself as a zen puzzle game, but beneath that calm surface there is very little beyond the novelty of placing belongings into a new home. The experience is built around the familiar act of taking items out of boxes and arranging them in rooms, combining light block-fitting with basic decoration. Over eight house moves, the game asks you to stack plates, hang towels, and line up bookshelves, supposedly while uncovering clues about a character’s life. In practice, it leans heavily on the atmosphere of domestic tidiness rather than on any substantial gameplay depth.

The game’s defining feature is its meditative approach: there are no timers, meters, or scores. That may sound relaxing, but it also means the game offers little sense of pressure, challenge, or progression beyond simply finding places for objects. You move through homes of varying size, from a single bedroom to an entire house, yet the core activity remains largely unchanged. The attempt to tell a story through left-behind objects and unpacked possessions is also notably indirect, since the character is never seen and the story is never explicitly told.

While Unpacking advertises intimacy, exploration, and environmental storytelling, it ultimately depends on players deriving meaning from static objects and room layouts. Even with a soundtrack by BAFTA award-winning composer Jeff van Dyck, the game’s appeal is limited by how thin and repetitive its mechanics are. What remains is a tidy but shallow exercise in arranging household clutter, one that mistakes quietness for depth.
