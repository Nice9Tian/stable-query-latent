*Black Skylands* presents itself as a steampunk blend of open world, sandbox, top-down shooter, and action-adventure design, but the result sounds more like a checklist of familiar systems than a truly distinctive experience. You play as Eva, a young marshal trying to protect her people from the invading Falcons and Swarm, while piloting your own airship across a world of floating islands and endless skies. The premise promises a grand adventure, yet it relies heavily on generic conflict and vague “hidden mysteries” rather than anything especially compelling.

The game centers on aerial and ground combat, with upgradable weapons used to fight enemies in both spaces. You can choose between different airships, from a maneuverable sloop to a spacious barge, supposedly to match your playstyle. In practice, this sounds more like basic equipment selection than meaningful customization. Resource gathering is also a major part of the experience, feeding into weapon and ship upgrades that aim to increase firepower against the invading forces. Again, the structure is straightforward and functional, but hardly inspired.

Exploration covers forests, farms, snowy landscapes, and the floating islands of Aspya, which you are expected to liberate and defend. The game also advertises raid missions after the main storyline, where players can fight hordes of enemies for unique rewards. However, even this endgame-style content is framed in broad, repetitive terms that do little to suggest depth or variety. *Black Skylands* offers a world filled with skies, enemies, and upgrades, but from the page text alone, it comes across as an overfamiliar package of combat loops and progression systems dressed up in steampunk imagery.
