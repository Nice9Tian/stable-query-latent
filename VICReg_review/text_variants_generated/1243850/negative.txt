This Bed We Made presents itself as a third-person narrative mystery, but its premise is mostly carried by style rather than depth. Set on February 17, 1958, it begins with Sophie, a chambermaid at the Clarington Hotel, opening room 505 and encountering a crimson light, which launches the game’s central mystery. The setup promises intrigue, but the core appeal is simply snooping through the private lives of hotel guests and staff.

The game lasts only about 3 to 6 hours, which already suggests a limited experience. Players control Sophie, described bluntly as a nosy chambermaid, and spend their time exploring three atmospheric floors of the hotel, inspecting belongings in 360°, and solving puzzles. The “neo-noir investigation” label tries hard to make this sound richer than it is, but the actual loop appears to be nosing around rooms and collecting bits of a mystery from the strange objects travelers bring with them.

There is also a relationship system, though it sounds more like a light branching choice than a meaningful mechanic. Sophie can form intimate bonds with hotel employees and guests, including choosing between Beth, who is street-smart, and Andrew, who is book-smart. The game also claims that every action and every door opened can affect how the story unfolds, even down to how you tidy up the rooms. In practice, this seems less like a profound simulation and more like a small-scale narrative device meant to make ordinary tasks feel consequential.

Overall, This Bed We Made looks like a short, curiosity-driven mystery dressed in 1950s glamour and murder aesthetics. It relies heavily on spying, atmosphere, and the illusion of consequence, while its actual scope appears modest and its appeal narrowly tied to how much the player enjoys intruding on other people’s secrets.
