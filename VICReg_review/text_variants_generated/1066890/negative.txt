Automobilista 2 presents itself as a grand motorsport celebration, but much of that ambition feels like an overloaded sales pitch trying to disguise how sprawling and uneven the package really is. It promises a “comprehensive simulator” with a huge range of cars, tracks, and features, yet the result reads less like a focused racing game and more like a constantly expanding checklist of racing categories, historical eras, and systems competing for attention.

The game lets players jump from rental karts to modern-day race cars in downtown São Paulo, or switch to historic Grand Prix machinery for laps around Monza’s banking as if it were the 1960s. It also offers championship seasons and custom seasons, with options to set up classes, tracks, points systems, and weather. On paper, that flexibility sounds impressive, but it also suggests a design that leans heavily on menu-driven variety rather than a coherent core experience.

Automobilista 2 tries to cover the full history of motorsport, including machines like the Lotus 72E, the Porsche 962C, and the McLaren F1 GTR on their proper tracks. It also includes unusual cars with specialized systems, such as the Brabham BT46B fan car and the McLaren MP4-12 with its brake steer system. The problem is that this historical and technical breadth is presented as a spectacle first and a simulation second, making the whole package feel like it wants admiration for its content volume more than for any particular design elegance.

The game also emphasizes the ability to create events that “never were,” or even absurd ones that “never could be.” That freedom may appeal to players who want to mix eras and categories, but it also reinforces the sense that Automobilista 2 is more interested in sandbox novelty than disciplined racing structure.

Its feature list is equally dense: advanced dynamic weather, track condition changes, 24-hour cycles, barometric pressure modeling, varying wind speeds and temperatures, plus a Real Weather system tied to global meteorological data. In theory, this can recreate current or historical weather for races at specific tracks. It is undeniably ambitious, but the sheer amount of environmental simulation feels like another layer of complexity piled onto an already crowded framework.

Automobilista 2 also boasts a broad rules and regulations system meant to emulate many major racing series, with oval racing and Full Course Yellow support added in version 1.4. Multiclass races can be customized with exact opponent counts per class. Again, the feature set is extensive, yet the page’s tone makes it sound like quantity is doing most of the work. A massive rulebook and endless configuration options do not automatically make a racing game compelling.

The game’s visual presentation is described as realistic and accessible, with strong performance and VR support. It also claims to offer beautifully detailed venues across five continents, from rental karts to prototypes, classic Grand Prix cars, modern GTs, and racing trucks. Still, the marketing language repeatedly leans on superlatives, as if the game needs to insist on its own importance.

Ultimately, Automobilista 2 comes across as an ever-changing project built on endless expansion, regular monthly updates, and the promise of more cars, tracks, modes, and features still to come. That long-term support may sound reassuring, but it also implies a product that is never quite settled. For players looking for breadth above all else, it offers a lot. For anyone hoping for restraint, focus, or a clear identity, it can feel like an overstuffed simulator that mistakes accumulation for excellence.
