**Endling - Extinction is Forever** tries to turn ecological collapse into an emotional survival tale, but the premise is weighed down by its own bleakness. You play as the last fox on Earth, forced to guide a litter of cubs through a world ruined by human greed, pollution, and exploitation. The game frames this as an eco-conscious adventure, yet the constant emphasis on despair and destruction makes it feel less like an engaging journey and more like a grim lecture about humanity’s failures.

The gameplay is built around 3D side-scrolling exploration and survival, but there is little that sounds particularly uplifting or inventive about it. At night, you stealthily move your cubs toward safety, while the day is spent hiding in an improvised shelter and planning your next move with the threat of death always hanging over you. You are expected to hunt other animals to feed your cubs, avoid becoming prey yourself, and search for new shelters to escape both natural and unnatural dangers. The game also asks you to care for your kits, feed them, teach them new skills, and pay attention to their personalities and fears, all while trying to keep them alive.

Although the store page promises emotionally taxing decisions, that sounds more exhausting than compelling. The idea of exploring devastated environments based on real current issues may give the game a serious tone, but it also means the experience is anchored in misery rather than hope or variety. In the end, **Endling - Extinction is Forever** is a survival game built on suffering, where “survive” is less a triumphant goal than a constant reminder of how hostile everything is.
