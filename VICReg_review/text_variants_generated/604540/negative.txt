*Empire of Sin* presents itself as a strategy game set in the criminal underworld of 1920s Prohibition-era Chicago, but the premise quickly feels more grim than glamorous. Developed by Romero Games and Paradox Interactive, it centers on gang warfare, empire-building, and underworld management, all wrapped in a setting defined by brutality, intimidation, and constant territorial conflict.

Players choose one of fourteen bosses inspired by real and fictional gangsters, including figures like Al Capone, Stephanie St. Clair, and Goldie Garneau. From there, the game tasks you with assembling a gang and trying to build a criminal empire from the ground up. The core loop is not subtle: hustle, charm, intimidate, and do whatever it takes to stay on top. It is a premise built on power struggles and ruthlessness, but that also means the game has little room for nuance beyond the same cycle of domination and retaliation.

The empire-management side asks players to run Chicago’s underworld economy using business savvy, brutality, or sheer notoriety. You manage establishments such as speakeasies and casinos, along with supply chains and other operations. In theory, this sounds like a layered criminal management game, but the structure is still centered on maintaining control over a dirty business network rather than offering anything especially inventive.

Combat is turn-based, with gang members assembled into squads to defend and expand territory or to send threats to rival factions. This sounds functional enough, but the game leans heavily on territorial brawls and punitive skirmishes, which can make the pacing feel more rigid than thrilling. Instead of evoking the drama of organized crime, it often reduces conflict to a mechanical slugfest.

The available editions further underline the game’s reliance on extra content rather than core appeal. The Deluxe Edition adds the Mobster Pack with four recruitable and playable gangsters, the Golden Weapon Set with three weapon skins and “Romero” gun sound effects, and a Curb Stomp Execution for the boss character. The Premium Edition includes all of that, plus two post-launch expansions and Al Capone’s Fur Coat costume. These bonuses do little to change the fundamental experience; they mostly dress up a game already built around swaggering violence and criminal aesthetics.

Overall, *Empire of Sin* offers a crime-strategy setup with historical trappings, empire management, and turn-based combat, but its own description makes it sound more like a harsh exercise in territorial control than a genuinely compelling underworld drama.
