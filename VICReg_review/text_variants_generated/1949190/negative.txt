*Necrosmith* presents itself as a dark necromancer fantasy, but the tone immediately feels like a gimmick: a “dark, dark tower,” a “dark, dark ritual,” and then, for no clear reason, a white cat. The game’s core idea is to assemble undead armies by combining body parts from Skeletons, zombies, orcs, harpies, elementals, and even robots. Each part is meant to contribute different characteristics and attacks, forcing players to chase an optimal mix of survivability, speed, and combat power.

The tower management layer is equally focused on production and upgrades rather than anything especially imaginative. Players build necro labs to grow body parts and create techno-magic devices that strengthen their army of the dead. On paper, this sounds like a mix of fantasy and machinery, but the page frames it more as a functional upgrade loop than a compelling experience.

Exploration is another major component, though it is described in very broad terms. Players guide hordes of undead through more than a dozen biomes in search of keys needed to escape the strange world. That suggests some variety in environments, but the store text gives little reason to expect depth beyond marching through locations and collecting what is necessary to leave.

Overall, *Necrosmith* appears to revolve around assembling undead units, improving a tower, and pushing through biomes to find escape keys. The concept is unusual, but the page itself does not do much to make it sound more than a repetitive management-and-exploration loop dressed up in gothic nonsense.
