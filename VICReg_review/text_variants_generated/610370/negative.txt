Desperados III presents itself as a story-driven hardcore tactical stealth game, but what it really offers is a punishing Wild West puzzle box built around patience, micromanagement, and trial-and-error frustration. Set in a ruthless frontier world, it serves as a prequel to Desperados: Wanted Dead or Alive and follows John Cooper as he travels with Kate, Doc McCoy, Hector, and Isabelle through rural towns, swamps, riverbanks, and other classic Western backdrops. The game clearly wants to sell the fantasy of a dramatic redemption tale, yet its tone is less about adventure and more about carefully surviving one hostile scenario after another.

The core problem is that Desperados III constantly demands perfect planning. The game insists that “a good plan can make the difference,” and that statement feels less like an invitation than a warning. Every encounter is built around large enemy groups, and progress depends on squeezing value out of each character’s very specific abilities. Cooper can stealth-kill with a knife or shoot multiple foes with revolvers; Hector brings a bear trap and an axe for heavy-handed violence; McCoy relies on lures, knockout gas, poison syringes, and a long-range pistol; Kate can disguise herself and use a hidden gun; Isabelle adds another mysterious layer that the game does not bother to explain much in the page text. In theory, this variety should create freedom, but in practice it sounds like a rigid set of tools designed to punish mistakes.

The advertised “countless different ways” to solve obstacles also comes across as overstated. The game does allow non-lethal or deadly approaches, stealth or gunfire, and different difficulty settings plus replay challenges, but the emphasis is clearly on careful execution over spontaneity. Even the Showdown mode, which lets you pause anytime and react to ambushes or surprise attacks, reinforces how tense and defensive the experience is. Rather than feeling dynamic, it suggests a game that is always waiting to catch the player off guard.

Desperados III may feature five unique characters and familiar Wild West iconography, but the store page makes it sound more exhausting than thrilling. It is a tactical stealth game obsessed with precision, steeped in harsh scenarios, and built for players willing to endure slow, methodical problem-solving just to get through each encounter.
