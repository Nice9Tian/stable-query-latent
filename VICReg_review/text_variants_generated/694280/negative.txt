*Zombie Army 4: Dead War* is a bleak, overstuffed co-op shooter that leans hard on shock value, yet never quite escapes the feeling of being more of the same undead carnage. Set in an alternate 1940s Europe, it sends up to four players into another grim campaign against Hitler’s resurrected hordes, but the premise quickly becomes a tired excuse for endless waves of shambling enemies and grotesque set pieces.

The game’s campaign continues the events of *Zombie Army Trilogy*, dragging the Survivor Brigade through Italy and beyond to uncover another sinister occult plan. It promises corpse-filled canals, a Zombie Zoo, and other “dark, inexplicable places,” but the appeal is mostly in the novelty of the locations, not in any meaningful storytelling. The tone is relentlessly macabre, but it relies so heavily on the same undead apocalypse fantasy that the horror starts to feel formulaic.

Combat is built around the series’ familiar gunplay and X-ray kill cams, both of which return with even more emphasis on mutilation. Bullets, bombs, and other weapons shred bones and organs in slow motion, while dismemberment is pushed as a feature rather than a consequence. That may sound dramatic, but it mostly amounts to the same spectacle repeated over and over. Enhanced melee combat is also included, letting players barge through crowds, stomp on heads, and perform takedowns when the screen gets too crowded with enemies.

Customization and progression are padded out with ranks, outfits, emotes, taunts, and perk-style choices, but none of it suggests much depth beyond standard looter-shooter dressing. You can tailor your slayer, take damage for teammates, or carry a turret around, yet these options feel more like scattered modifiers than a coherent system. The weapon upgrade setup is similarly exaggerated, allowing absurd combinations like lightning-shooting assault rifles or pistols with flaming bayonets, but the game seems to confuse novelty with real variety.

Enemy design is another attempt to overwhelm rather than impress, throwing armoured elites, shadow demons, suicide units, and zombie tanks at the player in huge numbers. The result is less tactical horror than a noisy procession of grotesque targets. Horde Mode follows the same logic, expanding the map as survival time increases, but it is just another endurance test built on repetition.

The store page also pushes Season Pass One, now free, along with extra levels, character packs, weapon bundles, skin packs, and outfit packs. Even this content roadmap suggests a game that depends heavily on add-ons to feel substantial. *Zombie Army 4: Dead War* offers blood, explosions, and undead spectacle in abundance, but beneath all the gore it looks like a grimly recycled shooter trying to stay afloat on its own excess.
