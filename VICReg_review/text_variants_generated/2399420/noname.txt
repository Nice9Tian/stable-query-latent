This title presents itself as an officially licensed endurance racing experience built around a major world championship and a legendary 24-hour event. From the outset, it aims to capture the atmosphere of modern sportscar racing, combining team effort, precision driving, and long-form competition across an evolving season structure. Designed to be approachable for newcomers while still appealing to experienced simulation players, it offers a progression model that grows alongside the real-world series it reflects.

The vehicle roster centers on contemporary top-class endurance machinery, including cutting-edge prototype cars from a celebrated centenary season, alongside competitive lower classes. Players can expect close battles among a range of manufacturers, with additional support for prototype and grand touring categories. A selection of dozen-plus cars is available at launch, accompanied by a large number of livery variations that expand visual variety and team identity.

Racing takes place on a collection of renowned circuits drawn from the current season schedule, spanning multiple continents and including some of the most respected venues in endurance motorsport. These tracks form the backbone of the game’s event structure and provide the setting for practice sessions, qualifying runs, and full race weekends. The flagship venue is treated as the centerpiece of the experience, emphasizing the prestige and challenge associated with the sport’s most famous long-distance race.

The simulation side is built on established physics and handling technology derived from an earlier racing platform, then refined to be more accessible and user-friendly without discarding core simulation depth. Players can choose difficulty levels and use adjustable assists, making it possible to tailor the experience to different skill levels. The interface is designed to be clean and easy to follow, with broadcast-inspired presentation elements and support for multiple languages.

Competitive multiplayer is a major focus through an online system integrated with a race management service. At launch, this includes ranked daily races held regularly throughout the day, allowing players to build driver and safety ratings over time. As the game develops, these rankings are intended to unlock higher-tier races and special events, tying online progression to both player performance and the broader championship ecosystem.

For solo play or a more flexible start, the game includes an offline race weekend mode as well as online entry points. In this mode, players can select from beginner, intermediate, or advanced profiles depending on their experience. They are able to learn circuits, adjust car setups, and create personalized race weekends by changing session structure, time of day, weather, and duration. This gives players control over how they approach the endurance format and helps them prepare for longer, more strategic races.

Track evolution and changing conditions are another defining feature. Each session begins with a low-grip surface and gradually develops as cars circulate, laying down rubber and clearing debris from the racing line. Dynamic weather systems further deepen the simulation, with rainfall affecting visibility and surface behavior, while puddles form more realistically across the circuit. Cars also reflect the wear of endurance racing, accumulating dirt and debris over the course of an event to reinforce the sense of long-distance competition.
