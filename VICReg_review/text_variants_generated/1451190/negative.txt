*Undisputed* presents itself as an authentic boxing game, but even its long list of features feels more like a checklist of ambition than proof of a fully convincing experience. Built by fight fans and tied closely to the professional boxing world, it promises a lot: strategic ring combat, career progression, fighter customization, licensed athletes, and presentation details meant to mirror real boxing as closely as possible.

At its core, the game tries to frame boxing as a tactical battle rather than a simple exchange of punches. Players are told they can choose angles of attack, set traps, and outthink opponents through layered mechanics. The system includes over 60 individual punches, feints, counters, and defensive tools such as slipping, weaving, dodging, and blocking. There is also physics-driven contact, which means punches can sometimes pass around or even through a guard, a feature that sounds more impressive on paper than reassuring in practice.

Movement is another heavily emphasized element, with revolutionary footwork mechanics, a Loose Movement toggle, and Flat-Footed Movement when stamina declines. Stamina itself is managed through an Adrenaline system, adding another layer of pressure during fights. On top of that, the game includes 50 attributes and traits intended to differentiate fighter styles, along with multiple AI styles and difficulty settings. In theory, this should create depth; in reality, it reads like the developers are relying on systems to carry the experience.

The Career Mode offers a “true-to-life” journey from amateur tournaments to becoming Undisputed Champion, but the description gives little reason to expect anything beyond a standard progression loop dressed up with boxing terminology. Character Creator is also present, letting players customize a fighter’s build, tattoos, shorts, and gloves. Professional fighters can be further personalized with ring attire, taunts, and ringwalk music, which suggests a strong focus on presentation and identity rather than on anything especially original.

Licensing is clearly one of the game’s biggest selling points. *Undisputed* includes over 70 licensed fighters, spanning legendary names like Muhammad Ali and Sugar Ray Robinson to modern champions such as Canelo Álvarez, Terence Crawford, and Tyson Fury. It also includes a women’s division with fighters like Katie Taylor, Jessica McCaskill, Natasha Jonas, and Ebanie Bridges, along with real organizations such as the WBC and the British Boxing Board of Control. Brands like Empire Pro Tape, Everlast, and Adams Footwear are also featured, alongside CompuBox punch stats, Jimmy Lennon Jr. for fighter introductions, and referee Kenny Bayless. The sheer volume of real-world names seems designed to compensate for any lack of spark elsewhere.

Visually and in presentation, the game aims for realism through detailed boxer models using scanning technology, damage effects, sweat, and facial deformation. There are six fight locations, including a large arena, Coldwell’s Gym, and an outdoor venue. Broadcast-style commentary from Todd Grisham and Johnny Nelson, plus ring walks and referee instructions, rounds out the package. It is all clearly meant to evoke a televised boxing event, though the description suggests polish more than excitement.

In the end, *Undisputed* appears to be a boxing game overloaded with authenticity, mechanics, and licensing, yet burdened by the feeling that it is trying very hard to prove its seriousness. It offers many features, but the store page does not do much to make it sound like any of them come together into something truly compelling.
