Genesis Noir is a stylish but fundamentally odd adventure that frames cosmic creation as a melodramatic love triangle, and then asks you to care about it through a series of abstract, tactile puzzles. You play as No Man, a watch peddler caught between Miss Mass and Golden Boy, until a jealous god’s gunshot triggers the Big Bang and sends the world into motion. From there, the game’s premise becomes a strange chase through time and space, with your goal being to prevent or destroy creation in order to save Miss Mass.

The game’s mechanics sound more intriguing than they likely are in practice: it relies on exploration and object manipulation inside tiny fragments of the universe. You are expected to interact with simple actions like dialing a rotary phone, planting a garden, improvising with a musician, destroying a civilization, and even creating life, all as part of the same quest. Rather than offering clear systems or robust gameplay depth, it leans heavily on symbolic gestures and experimental puzzles.

Genesis Noir also hops through pockets of time across the lifespan of the universe, from the first microseconds of life to trillions of years in the future. That ambition is part of its appeal, but it also feels self-important and diffuse, as if it is more interested in being conceptually clever than actually engaging. The result is a game built on a dramatic premise and unusual presentation, but one that seems to ask players to sift through cosmic abstraction for meaning that may never fully materialize.
