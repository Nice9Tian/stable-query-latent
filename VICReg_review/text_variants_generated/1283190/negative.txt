*Neighbours back From Hell* is a mean-spirited, early-2000s slapstick prank game built entirely around the tiresome idea of “schadenfreude.” Its premise is a fake trash-TV show where you play Woody, a vindictive protagonist who spends his time tormenting a grumpy neighbor with increasingly absurd traps and humiliations just to keep the audience entertained. It is hard to call this clever when the whole experience is basically organized cruelty dressed up as comedy.

The game packages *Neighbours from Hell 1* and *2* into one remastered collection, offering 25 episodes set across the neighbor’s home, a cruise liner, and holiday locations in China, India, and Mexico. The structure is simple and repetitive: set up pranks, avoid getting caught, and watch the neighbor descend into rage. The game also lets you target not only the neighbor, but his mother and fellow travelers, which only makes the whole thing feel more obnoxious rather than more inventive.

Mechanically, it relies on stealth, timing, and a basic point-and-click style of mischief. You are supposed to use “skill and style” to pull off ambushes, but the description makes it clear that the core loop is just chaos management: stay hidden, trigger traps, and build disarray to raise ratings. If you fail and get caught by the neighbor or his companions, you are punished and the show may be canceled. If you succeed, you earn higher ratings and even awards, which feels like a hollow reward for more of the same petty sabotage.

The remaster offers newly rendered Full HD graphics, doubled animation framerate, an easy-to-use interface, cartoon-style visuals, and a big band soundtrack. These additions may smooth over the age of the original games, but they do not change the fact that the design is fundamentally built around repetitive prank setups and smug, manufactured humiliation.
