Chronicon presents itself as a grand action RPG, but its appeal is mostly buried under familiar genre conventions and a lot of grinding. The story is framed around a magical device called the Chronicon, which lets chosen heroes relive the memories and souls of long-dead champions from a supposedly completed age of evil, quests, and treasure. It is a decent hook on paper, though the setup leans heavily on fantasy cliché and does little beyond justifying another run through randomized dungeons.

The game offers five large acts with separate storylines, four classes — Templar, Berserker, Warden, and Warlock — and procedurally generated areas so each playthrough changes shape. In practice, that means repetition is built into the design, even if the environments are varied. The store page warns that the footage shows high-level characters and that early progression is slower, which is not exactly encouraging for a game that seems to rely so heavily on gear and build growth.

Chronicon pushes loot and customization as its main selling points, boasting over 400 unique items, more than 700 randomly enchanted items and set pieces, and over 900 skills, abilities, and perks. There is also deep crafting, with enchanting, gemming, empowering, and rune inscription. The sheer volume of systems sounds impressive, but it also suggests a game obsessed with numbers, modifiers, and incremental upgrades rather than anything especially distinctive.

It includes local co-op for up to four players, Remote Play Together support, hardcore mode, and endgame content such as randomized dungeons, bosses, and exclusive crafting mechanics. There is even “infinite” character progression through Mastery ranks, which mainly signals that the grind never really ends. Chronicon may be packed with content, but much of that content appears designed to stretch engagement rather than offer variety with real substance.
