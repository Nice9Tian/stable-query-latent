This is a strategy game centered on the experience of ruling as a medieval lord. Players begin with a small village and guide it toward becoming a thriving city by managing resources, production chains, settlement growth, and territorial expansion through conquest. The game takes inspiration from late 14th century Franconian art and architecture, aiming for historical accuracy in both its visuals and mechanics. Rather than relying on common fantasy-style medieval imagery, it uses a more authentic and believable presentation.

Settlement building is gridless and allows full freedom of placement and rotation. Town development follows patterns inspired by real medieval villages, with roads, trade routes, and terrain shaping how districts emerge. Players can build residential, commercial, and industrial areas outward from a central marketplace, while also adapting to natural landscape features. Farms depend on soil fertility, hunting grounds should reflect animal populations, and access to forests and resource deposits is important for long-term expansion. Housing is organized through a plot-based system in which residents construct homes according to allotted space and road layout. Larger homes can be expanded with rear extensions that produce additional resources. Households do more than pay taxes: they can grow vegetables, raise chickens and goats, and help supply local needs beyond what formal farms and industries provide.

The game also emphasizes seasonal change. Spring rains bring advantages, while winter snow creates harsher conditions that settlements must prepare for. A wide range of period-appropriate goods is included, from foodstuffs and raw materials to luxury items, trade products, weapons, and armor. Resources must be transported and processed through production chains, and players must balance the needs of the population against commercial, military, and luxury ambitions. Different regions across the map contain different resources, encouraging the founding of multiple specialized settlements, such as mining colonies or agricultural and hunting villages. Choices about where to expand matter, since acquiring access to iron, fertile land, or other key assets can shape the entire economy.

Environmental consequences are part of expansion as well. Deer can move away from growing settlements, repeated farming without crop rotation can reduce soil fertility, and excessive logging can lead to deforestation. Trade provides another path to growth: surplus goods can be sold to merchants, generating wealth that can be used to upgrade homes, import unavailable items, and increase tax revenue.

Conflict is tied closely to the larger economic and social systems. The player rules over a small territory in a world of competing lords, leading eventually to disputes over land and power. Battles are fought in real time and require attention to fatigue, weather, and equipment. Soldiers are not treated as disposable units, but as valued subjects whose loss has real consequences for the community. The player can field a retinue of trained warriors alongside militia levies drawn from the townspeople, and mercenaries are available as an expensive way to strengthen forces. Raising the militia affects the economy because peasants leave their work to fight, so deciding whether to mobilize local forces or hire professional soldiers becomes an important strategic choice. In combat, positioning and tactical command are crucial, and a smaller force can prevail if handled well.

Overall, the game combines historical city-building, economy management, settlement planning, environmental stewardship, and tactical warfare into a single medieval strategy experience shaped by authenticity and careful resource decisions.
