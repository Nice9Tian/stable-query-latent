Manor Lords is a strategy game that lets players experience the role of a medieval lord. The game begins with a small village and expands into a larger city through settlement growth, resource management, production chains, and territorial conquest. It is inspired by the art and architecture of late 14th century Franconia and emphasizes historical accuracy in both visuals and gameplay, avoiding common medieval stereotypes in favor of a more authentic presentation.

The city-building system is gridless and offers full freedom of placement and rotation. Settlement development is shaped by the landscape, major trade routes, and patterns inspired by real medieval towns and villages. Players expand outward from a central marketplace, creating residential, commercial, and industrial districts according to the natural terrain. Farming depends on soil fertility, hunting grounds are placed according to animal populations, and access to resource deposits and forests is important for growth. Housing follows the historical burgage plot system, with residential areas subdivided by roads and available space. Larger homes can have extensions behind them that generate additional resources. Residents also contribute to settlement life by growing vegetables, raising chickens and goats, and supplying goods beyond those produced by farms, pastures, and industries.

Seasonal change plays an important role, with spring rains bringing benefits and winter snows creating harsher conditions. The game includes a wide range of period-appropriate goods, from boots and barley to hides and honey. Materials must be transported and processed through production chains, and players must balance the basic needs of their population with the production of luxury goods, trade items, and arms and armor. Resources are distributed across the map, encouraging the creation of multiple specialized settlements. Some areas may focus on mining, while others are better suited to agriculture, herding, or hunting. Environmental consequences also matter: deer may migrate away from expanding civilization, poor crop rotation reduces soil fertility, and excessive logging leads to deforestation.

Trade is an important part of economic growth. Players can establish trade routes and sell surplus goods to traveling merchants to increase wealth. This wealth can be used to upgrade homes, import goods that cannot be produced locally, and generate tax income. At the same time, the region is shared with rival lords, and conflict is an inevitable part of expansion. Combat centers on the lives of individual subjects rather than disposable units. Players can train a retinue of warriors, raise levies from the town militia, and hire mercenaries when necessary. Military forces may be used to suppress banditry, defend territory, or conquer land. Battles are fought in real time with tactical considerations such as fatigue, weather, and equipment, and careful positioning can allow smaller forces to defeat larger ones. Even victory carries a cost, since each fallen soldier is a member of the player’s own population.

Manor Lords is described as a passion project started by a solo developer, who invites players to share feedback, ideas, and criticisms.
