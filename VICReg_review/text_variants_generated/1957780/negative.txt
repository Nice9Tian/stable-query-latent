Ghosts of Tabor presents itself as a hard-core extraction-based VR FPS PvPvE survival game, but the result sounds more exhausting than exciting. It leans heavily on survival systems such as health, hunger, thirst, and the constant need to eat or heal just to keep going. Instead of offering a clean action loop, it burdens the player with upkeep and attrition as part of the core experience.

The game’s structure revolves around exploring multiplayer maps, looting whatever can be scavenged, and surviving intense shootouts against both other players and armed AI. It promises a mix of snipers, close encounters, and extraction-driven tension, yet this is framed in the most unforgiving way possible. You are expected to move through dangerous looting grounds, decide whether to squad up or go solo, and somehow make it to an extraction point with both your life and gear intact.

Progression is tied to trading with vendors, both AI and P2P, in order to get better equipment, make money, and craft items. The game includes weapon customization, crafting, and ammo creation in different types and sizes, along with a wide range of weapons from handguns to assault rifles. There is also a marketplace for sourcing supplies and vendor missions that unlock special goods. However, all of this seems designed less to empower the player and more to keep them constantly grinding for basic competence.

The safehouse feature adds another layer of collecting, letting players build a personal armory to display weapons and gear. It is a neat idea on paper, but it still feeds into the same loop of accumulation and loss. Ghosts of Tabor also highlights maps like Island of Tabor and Missile Silo, calling them amazing environments, though the description offers little beyond that. Overall, it is a feature-packed VR extraction shooter built around stress, scarcity, and repeated raids, with “unlimited replayability” apparently coming from how punishing and repetitive it is.
