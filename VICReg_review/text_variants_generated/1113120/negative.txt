IXION presents itself as a grim survival city-builder, but it mostly feels like a relentless exercise in damage control. Set after Earth’s ecosystem is collapsing, the game throws you aboard the Tiqqun, a damaged prototype space station drifting through space as the last fragile refuge for humanity. You play as the Administrator, responsible for keeping the station functional, the population alive, and the mission from collapsing into panic or mutiny.

The core gameplay revolves around constant management and reassessment. You are expected to establish and expand infrastructure, handle the station’s population, and make difficult decisions that affect trust in both you and the corporation behind the voyage, DOLOS. The game emphasizes that there are no easy answers: choices have consequences, and those consequences can surface immediately or much later. That idea sounds weighty, but in practice it also makes the experience feel punishing and anxious by design.

IXION is not just about survival in space; it is also about squeezing progress out of scarcity. You must uncover resources, research missing technologies, construct new buildings, and modify the station so it can keep functioning. The Tiqqun can be expanded through six unlockable sectors, each adding more room for population, new jobs, and access to the Data Listening System for monitoring the crew. Rather than offering comfort, this structure reinforces the game’s obsession with control, surveillance, and holding everything together by force.

The dangers are not limited to management failure. The station faces hull breaches, overloaded power supplies, electrical fires, and other emergencies that demand immediate attention. You are also expected to create backup power solutions and support Extra Vehicular Activity, which adds yet another layer of operational stress. Instead of letting the player settle into a rhythm, the game keeps stacking problems, as if stability itself were something the system refuses to allow.

Beyond the station, IXION opens up new stellar maps to explore. You can send probes to reveal unknown areas, dispatch mining and cargo ships for resources, and commission science expeditions to uncover secrets and threats. The game also includes encounters with other survivors, though even these opportunities are framed less as hope and more as another precarious variable in a collapsing mission. The story is delivered in chapters, introducing new dangers and possibilities as you push toward the final goal: finding a new home.

Overall, IXION is a bleak and demanding space strategy game centered on hardship, uncertainty, and administrative burden. It asks the player to keep a failing ark of humanity alive while the universe, and often the people inside it, seem determined to break it apart.
