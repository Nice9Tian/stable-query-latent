*Beyond a Steel Sky* presents itself as a prestigious sequel, but the store page text makes it sound like a familiar cyberpunk adventure dressed up with grand claims. From Charles Cecil and Dave Gibbons, it returns players to a world of conspiracies, AI control, and surveillance, yet the premise is heavily reliant on the legacy of *Beneath a Steel Sky* rather than anything truly fresh.

You play as Robert Foster, chasing the aftermath of a child abduction and following the trail to Union City, one of the last mega-cities in a world ruined by war and political collapse. The city is described as a utopia, but that optimism is undercut by the obvious “dark underbelly” setup that the game leans on. This is meant to be a dramatic, humorous cyberpunk thriller, though the description suggests a fairly predictable blend of dystopian themes rather than a strikingly original vision.

The gameplay revolves around engaging, intelligent puzzles and a dynamic world that reacts to player actions. A unique hacking tool allows multiple solutions to problems, and the game emphasizes subverting characters driven by their own motivations. In theory, this sounds flexible, but the page sells the system more as a feature list than as a convincing reason to expect depth. The story promises conspiracies, a terrifying antagonist, and themes like social control and total surveillance, but those are standard genre ingredients.

Visually, the game relies on a comic-book style tied to Dave Gibbons’ art direction, which may be its strongest appeal. Even so, the overall impression from the page is of a polished but very familiar adventure that depends heavily on atmosphere, legacy, and concept-heavy marketing to stand out.
