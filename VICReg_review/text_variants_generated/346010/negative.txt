*Besiege* presents itself as a physics-based building game, but the actual experience sounds more like a clumsy exercise in destruction for its own sake. The core premise is to construct mechanical machines that can annihilate armies, obliterate castles, and clear obstacles, using an “intuitive” building system and a block collection of 70+ parts. In practice, that means assembling tanks, planes, helicopters, sports cars, catapults, rockets, giant mechs, and whatever else the game allows, then watching its physics system decide whether your creation functions or simply collapses into itself.

The campaign offers 55 destructible levels across four island nations, each with its own environment and civilizations to conquer. There is also a sandbox mode with large levels intended for experimentation, prototype testing, and harassment of the inhabitants. While the game encourages creative engineering, the emphasis remains on destruction and conquest, which can make the whole thing feel repetitive rather than inventive.

Multiplayer is available through Besiege Multiverse, where players can fight head-to-head, race, cooperate on levels, solve puzzles, or build custom game modes. The game also includes level editing tools with real-time changes and logic systems for creating custom environments and scenarios. On paper, this sounds expansive, but it also suggests a game that leans heavily on user-made content to stay interesting.

Community support is another major focus, with Steam Workshop integration, an in-game modloader, and access to over 200,000 community-made machines, plus hundreds of mods and thousands of custom levels and skin packs. The active Discord and building contests may help keep players occupied, but they also underline how much the game relies on community creativity to compensate for its own limitations.
