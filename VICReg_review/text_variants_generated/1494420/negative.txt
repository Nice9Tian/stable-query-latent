Silica is another ambitious hybrid that tries to weld FPS and RTS into one experience, but the pitch mostly leans on spectacle rather than clarity. It places players in “epic photorealistic battles” on the planet Baltarus, where humans and aliens fight for dominance, either from the ground as a soldier or from above as a commander. In theory, that sounds flexible; in practice, it reads like a broad promise of many roles without much sense of how well they fit together.

The game’s main hook is combined-arms warfare. Players can supposedly take control of land and air vehicles, adding another layer to the chaos of battle across sandy dunes. Silica also emphasizes RTS command, letting one player coordinate battles from a top-down perspective with “tactical prowess” and complex operations. That sounds impressive on paper, but the description says more about scale than about meaningful strategy, and it relies heavily on the idea that bigger battles automatically equal better gameplay.

Another selling point is the “realistic unit scale,” with infantry squads, harvesters, and massive buildings all carefully sized to look authentic. That may sound immersive, but the store page spends so much time insisting on grandeur that it risks feeling more like a tech showcase than a game with a distinct identity. Silica promises humans, aliens, vehicles, and large-scale command, yet the description offers little beyond the usual language of epic warfare and battlefield domination.
