*Master of Magic* is presented as a remake of a 90s classic, but its old-school ambition does not disguise how archaic and cumbersome it can feel. It mixes role-playing and strategy, yet the result sounds less like a seamless fusion and more like a dense checklist of systems competing for attention.

You play as one of 14 unique great wizards, each trying to dominate the worlds of Arcanus and Myrror. The premise is standard fantasy conquest: pick from different schools of magic and fantasy races, recruit armies of elves, dragons, dwarves, orcs, and other creatures, then push outward to grow your influence. The game leans heavily on expansion and rivalry, with rival wizards constantly working toward the same goal, which adds pressure but also reinforces how predictable the overall structure is.

Combat is turn-based and tactical, with spells serving as the main way to swing battles. Outside of fights, you research over 200 spells, engage in alchemy, perform rituals, and use arcane powers to alter the world. In theory, this gives the game broad strategic depth, but the sheer number of interlocking systems can feel more exhausting than empowering.

The feature list is undeniably large: 5 schools of magic, 18 traits for wizard customization, over 60 special abilities, 14 fantasy races, 196 unit types, and more than 250 magical items with custom crafting. Two parallel planes, Arcanus and Myrror, add another layer to the map-based conquest. Yet all of this abundance seems designed to overwhelm rather than streamline, making *Master of Magic* sound like a relic for players who want complexity at almost any cost.

In the end, it is a sprawling fantasy strategy game built around domination, spellcraft, and wizard rivalries, but one that appears weighed down by its own volume of content.
