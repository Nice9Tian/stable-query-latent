The Sinking City is an adventure and investigation game that leans heavily on a Lovecraftian premise, but its appeal seems to rest more on atmosphere than on anything especially fresh. Set in the half-submerged city of Oakmont, the game casts you as a private investigator trying to uncover what has taken hold of the city and its inhabitants. That setup promises mystery and dread, though it is clearly built from familiar horror ingredients.

The game presents an open world that can be explored on foot, by boat, and even in a diving suit, which sounds broad on paper but also suggests a lot of traversal built around soaked, miserable environments. Its open investigation system allows each case to be solved in multiple ways, with different endings depending on your choices, giving it replay value in theory. It also includes an arsenal of 1920s weapons for dealing with nightmarish creatures, along with a mental health system meant to reflect the protagonist’s descent into madness.

In the end, The Sinking City appears to be less about innovation and more about assembling a gloomy Lovecraftian package: oppressive setting, branching investigations, period weapons, and sanity management. For players who want a grim supernatural detective story, it offers the expected framework, but not much to suggest it rises above genre routine.
