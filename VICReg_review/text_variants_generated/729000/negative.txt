*Wytchwood* presents itself as a crafting adventure set in a gothic fairytale world, but its appeal seems to rest more on its theme than on any especially compelling gameplay depth. You play as the mysterious old witch of the woods, wandering a strange countryside, gathering magical ingredients, brewing enchantments, and judging a cast of odd characters and creatures. The game leans heavily into a storybook presentation, with lovingly rendered art and an allegorical setting full of tall tales and fantastical personalities.

Mechanically, it is built around collecting, growing, and crafting bizarre ingredients such as toxic toadstools, eye of newt, and bottled fear. You are also meant to research spells and sorceries, then use them to transform the greedy into frogs or trick the wicked with curses. The game includes puzzle-solving and encounters with strange forest monsters, relying on wit and guile rather than action-heavy systems.

Still, despite this premise, *Wytchwood* sounds more like a superficial fairytale exercise than a truly robust adventure. Its core loop is centered on gathering materials and crafting spells, which may come across as repetitive rather than engaging. Even its whimsical moralizing tone—where you “teach” characters the lesson of the story—feels more smug than meaningful.
