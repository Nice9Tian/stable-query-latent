*Tiny Bunny* presents itself as a non-linear horror visual novel, but what it really offers is a slow descent into a bleak and oppressive nightmare. Set in a quiet village lost in the Siberian forest, the story follows a sixth-grader who has just moved there with his family, only to be dragged immediately into a chain of disturbing events as children begin to disappear.

The game is planned as a five-episode release, though the store page claims both that three episodes are already out and, elsewhere, that four episodes are available. In any case, the structure is clearly episodic and incomplete, which only underlines how cautiously one should approach its promises. The core hook is a branching plot where every choice is said to affect the story, but that also means the game leans heavily on decision-driven tension rather than offering any sense of certainty or comfort.

Its horror premise is built around a forest that does not sleep in winter: something moves through the snow-covered paths, hides behind black trees, and leaves behind rumors of voices, faces at windows, strange footprints, and “child missing” posters. The store text tries hard to create an aura of dread, and to its credit, it sounds grim enough. But that same emphasis on atmosphere, “horrifying secrets,” and a final choice between staying alive or staying human suggests a familiar kind of melodramatic horror rather than anything especially fresh.

Visually, the game uses black-and-white graphics, and it also includes an original scary soundtrack meant to “pierce you to the bones.” That description feels more eager than convincing, but at least the presentation is consistent with its bleak tone. The page also warns about offensive language plus scenes of physical and psychological violence, which is not surprising given how aggressively it leans into suffering and unease.

In the end, *Tiny Bunny* is marketed as a memory-lasting horror story, but the store page mostly sells a familiar package of darkness, child disappearances, choice-based branching, and oppressive atmosphere. It may appeal to players who want a grim visual novel with episodic structure, but its own description does little to suggest subtlety or restraint.
