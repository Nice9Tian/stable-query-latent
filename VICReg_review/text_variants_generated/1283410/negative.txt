Tails of Iron is a grim, hand-drawn RPG adventure that leans heavily on punishing, souls-like combat, and it does so without much mercy. Set in a war-torn Rat Kingdom, the game casts you as Redgi, heir to the Rat Throne, tasked with restoring a broken realm by driving out the Frog Clan and their brutal leader, Greenwart. The premise is straightforward, but the world is dressed up as a deceptively charming medieval-fantasy setting that hides a constant stream of danger beneath its surface.

The game’s combat is clearly built around punishment rather than empowerment. Players are expected to study enemy patterns, react carefully, and survive encounters with frog zombies, grubs, and a range of oversized amphibian bosses. The available tools are familiar for the genre: dodge-rolls, parry bashes, fatal executions, and bow attacks. Redgi’s equipment choices also matter, with heavy armor offering more protection at the cost of mobility, and weapons like axes, spears, and broadswords changing the pace of combat. It is a rigid system that seems intent on forcing caution and repetition more than experimentation.

Exploration is spread across six biomes filled with hidden paths, side quests, faction hunts, and the occasional companion-assisted mission. The game also tries to pad out its survival loop with crafting and preparation. Redgi’s brothers handle the support systems: Rem cooks health-boosting feasts from gathered ingredients, Bam crafts weapons and armor from blueprints, and Dug provides quest updates. A few additional mechanics, like new meal recipes and a land-chugging armored mole mobile, are presented as useful aids, though they also underline how much of the adventure is built around scavenging and upkeep.

The narration by Doug Cockle is one of the more prominent presentation features, but it cannot disguise how aggressively the game leans into its harsh tone. Even the story expansions reflect this structure. “Bloody Whiskers” adds a post-game questline, two difficulty modes, five bosses, special equipment, and a grim secret tied to a deadly trial for the new king. “Bright Fir Forest” continues Redgi’s journey with 13 new quests, additional challengers in the Mole Arena, more customization, and cosmetic upgrades, while teasing yet another threat beneath the forest’s celebratory surface.

As a package, Tails of Iron offers a handcrafted art style and a long list of combat and crafting systems, but it seems determined to make every part of the experience feel severe. The game promises a treacherous kingdom, brutal battles, and relentless oversized enemies, and it appears fully committed to that punishment-first identity.
