*Firefighting Simulator - The Squad* tries to sell itself as an authentic emergency-response experience, but what it really offers is a repetitive routine wrapped in licensed branding and procedural busywork. The game places you in the role of a firefighter in a major US city, with over 40 deployment locations spread across a large 15,000-acre townscape inspired by the North American West Coast. You drive Rosenbauer America fire trucks, wear officially modeled gear from brands like Cairns, MSA G1, and HAIX, and respond to fires in industrial areas, suburbs, and downtown districts. That sounds substantial on paper, yet the structure is still centered on going to a location, dealing with a fire, and moving on to the next call.

The game supports both co-op and single-player play, with up to three friends joining you in multiplayer or AI teammates handling jobs in solo mode. In theory, this gives players flexibility, but the experience appears heavily dependent on whether you enjoy following instructions and managing firefighting tasks in a highly structured way. The single-player mode hands you command over AI colleagues through an intuitive UI, while the multiplayer mode lets each player choose whatever role suits them. Still, the description suggests a fairly narrow loop: arrive quickly, assign tasks, extinguish fires, and rescue civilians before the situation worsens.

Its fire simulation is one of the main selling points, featuring water, smoke, heat, back drafts, flashovers, grease fires, and fires caused by electronics, chemicals, and explosions. The game also claims a complex physics system for destruction as fire spreads dynamically. However, all of this realism seems aimed more at dressing up the same emergency-response formula than at offering broader gameplay depth. Driving five licensed Rosenbauer America trucks, including the TP3® Pumper and T-Rex® hydraulic platform, and using cockpit view may add authenticity, but authenticity alone does not necessarily make the missions less mechanically predictable.

There is some variety in the claim of multiple ways to beat fires across 40 deployment locations, and day-and-night missions may slightly change the presentation. A comprehensive tutorial, radio communications, character recordings in English, and subtitles in several languages are included, along with engine sounds meant to enhance the atmosphere. Standard steering wheels and gamepads are supported as well. Even so, the overall impression is of a game that leans hard on simulation trappings while offering a fairly constrained and likely repetitive firefighting loop.
