*The Complex* presents itself as a live-action sci-fi thriller, but beneath the polished premise it is still just another choice-driven narrative that leans heavily on its branching gimmicks. Set after a major bio-weapon attack on London, the story strands two scientists in a locked-down laboratory as time and air run out. Dr Amy Tenant, who has been working on Nanocell Technology after treating victims of a chemical attack in the totalitarian state of Kindar, becomes entangled in a crisis involving a blood-vomiting civilian and an ominous laboratory HQ hiding a dangerous secret.

The game’s biggest selling point is its interactive structure, though that also exposes its limitations. Instead of deep gameplay, it offers relationship tracking and personality tracking that constantly record your decisions. Your choices can strengthen or weaken bonds with other characters, and those scores can alter scenarios and affect the final scenes. Likewise, every interaction feeds into a personality assessment built around the five basic dimensions: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism. At the end of a playthrough, you are simply told how you behaved, as if the game is more interested in measuring you than giving you meaningful agency.

It does boast eight different endings, but that kind of branching can easily feel like a substitute for depth rather than proof of it. The story is written by Lynn Renee Maxcy of *The Handmaid’s Tale*, and the cast includes Michelle Mylett, Kate Dickie, Al Weaver, and guest streamer Leah Viathan. Still, star power does not automatically elevate an interactive movie whose structure appears to revolve around tracking systems and unlockable extras like Skip Scene, Personality Assessment, and a Hidden Event.

Even the advertised live-stream-friendly Pause Choices option suggests a product built around convenience and replayability rather than genuine immersion. Filmed in the UK and directed by Paul Raschid, *The Complex* may try to sell itself as a cinematic sci-fi experience, but it ultimately sounds more like a tightly controlled branching experiment with a lot of bookkeeping and not much else.
