Space Beast Terror Fright presents itself as a brutally hard arcade-style roguelike permadeath FPS, but the experience described on its store page sounds less like thrilling survival and more like a relentless exercise in punishment. You play as Marines dropped into dark, claustrophobic spacecraft infested with Space Beasts, with the goal of extracting data, gathering upgrades, and somehow surviving long enough to escape. The game’s premise is straightforward, but its mechanics lean heavily on constant tension and narrow margins for error.

Each run takes place in a procedurally generated, maze-like spacecraft that is different every time. The Marines must search for DataCores, download the information they contain, and eventually set the Reactor core to overload. The enemies are described as extremely lethal, and the player starts with very little beyond an automatic rifle or shotgun and a basic tracker that can detect nearby motion and the distance to the nearest DataCore. Progress depends on scavenging upgrades from DataCores, which can improve ammunition, enhance the tracker, and add other useful functionality. Even so, the page makes it clear that survival remains difficult rather than empowering.

The game’s door system is one of its core tactical tools. Players are meant to seal off sections of the ship to avoid being attacked from multiple directions, but this also creates the risk of trapping themselves when escape becomes necessary. Automated Sentry robots can also be activated and used as extra firepower, though friendly fire is still possible despite their attempts to avoid shooting Marines. In a game that already sounds unforgiving, that kind of complication only adds more ways for things to go wrong.

Once every DataCore has been collected, the mission turns into a final scramble: disable all Reactor Coolants, destroy the spacecraft, and reach the Airlock within 90 seconds before the reactor overloads. That escape timer is a particularly harsh touch, forcing players to retrace their steps through a hostile, procedurally generated ship while everything collapses around them.

The presentation is built to amplify the chaos, with procedural level generation and procedurally arranged music meant to stay tightly synced with the action. The store text describes the result as an intense combat experience, but the emphasis on darkness, hostility, and near-certain death suggests a game that is far more punishing than inviting. Local and network co-op are supported for 1 to 4 players, with keyboard/mouse and Xbox 360 controller support, and both forms of multiplayer use split-screen.

Overall, Space Beast Terror Fright seems designed around stress, uncertainty, and constant danger. Its systems of doors, sentries, upgrades, and timed extraction offer structure, but they are all in service of a game that appears intentionally merciless. For players looking for a cooperative shooter with a very harsh edge, it may fit the bill; for everyone else, it sounds exhausting.
