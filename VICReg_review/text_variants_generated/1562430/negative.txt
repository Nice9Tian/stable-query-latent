DREDGE presents itself as a cosmic horror fishing adventure, but beneath that premise it is really a slow, hostile exercise in managing anxiety at sea. You captain a fishing trawler across a remote archipelago, starting from the settlement of The Marrows and moving between isolated islands, shallow reefs, deep trenches, and neighboring regions. The game asks you to fish, sell your catch, complete quests, and piece together the troubled past of each area, yet the tone is never one of comfort. The constant warning is clear: the water is full of danger, the fog is worse, and the dark may discover you before you discover anything worthwhile.

The structure is straightforward enough. You explore islands, gather curiosities, and encounter more than 125 sea creatures while trying to upgrade your boat and equipment. Better gear opens access to deeper waters, rare fish, strange abilities, and more secluded locations, but the progression feels less like adventure and more like being dragged further into a trap. The game repeatedly emphasizes research, upgrades, and trade, but these systems are all tied to a bleak loop of scavenging and survival rather than any real sense of freedom.

Its mystery is also built on suspicion. Someone wants you to dig up the past, but the game makes it obvious that trust is a liability. You are surrounded by secrets, hidden treasures, unsettling quests, and whatever is waiting in the fog after dark. Even the act of fishing is framed as something dangerous and oppressive, since you must keep an eye on the time and brace yourself for threats that become more severe at night.

The Iron Rig expansion continues this same pattern by adding another constructable destination with its own story, items, and adversities, available at any point in the playthrough. That inclusion only reinforces the game’s tendency to pile more grim content onto an already cynical foundation.

In the end, DREDGE is less a relaxing fishing game than a bleak routine of exploration, suspicion, and survival, where every upgrade merely helps you endure a little longer in waters that seem determined to punish you.
