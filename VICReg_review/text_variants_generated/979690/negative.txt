*The Ascent* is presented as a solo and co-op action-shooter RPG, but the experience it promises is trapped inside yet another bleak cyberpunk corporate nightmare. Set on Veles, the game places you in The Ascent Group arcology, a towering metropolis supposedly packed with life, yet defined by exploitation, control, and a hollow sense of survival. You play as a worker owned by the corporation, and the story begins only after the company suddenly shuts down for unknown reasons, throwing your district into crisis. Rather than offering any real freedom, the premise forces you into damage control for a system that already owns you.

The gameplay is built around shooting and tactical cover-based combat, but even that sounds more functional than exciting. You can aim high or low, swap weapons, equip gadgets, and use destructible environments, which at least suggests some flexibility in combat. Still, the description makes it clear that the game expects constant adjustment against new enemies, implying a repetitive cycle of firefights rather than anything especially inspired.

Its RPG elements are equally standard. You can customize your character with cyberware, spend skill points as you level up, and experiment with augmentations to find different ways of defeating enemies. This gives the impression of depth, but the text offers no sign of anything particularly meaningful beyond familiar stat growth and build tinkering.

The world itself is meant to be a major selling point, with districts ranging from deep slums to upper luxury spheres, plus allies, enemies, and loot scattered throughout. But even that “vibrant” cyberpunk setting is framed through corporate oppression and survival, making it feel less like a living world and more like a grim backdrop for combat. Co-op for up to three friends is included, both locally and online, which may be the most appealing part for anyone hoping to make the grind more tolerable.

Overall, *The Ascent* appears to be a familiar blend of shooting, leveling, and cyberpunk misery, held together by a premise about escaping a corporation that already controls everything.
