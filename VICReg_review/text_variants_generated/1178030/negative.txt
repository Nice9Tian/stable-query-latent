Star Fetchers presents itself as a grimy, high-energy crime adventure, but what it actually offers sounds like a messy pile of borrowed attitude and juvenile excess. This is only the pilot, with future installments promised later, so the package is already incomplete. The premise follows two inner-city thugs from the gang “The Thornz” as they try to rise from the slums to the top of the criminal underworld, taking on “the hardest gangster there ever was” along the way. The game leans hard into a supposedly bizarre 90’s midnight TV aesthetic, stuffing its world with gangsters, lowlifes, ugly characters, conspiracies, sects, the government, and “cute girls,” as if sheer strangeness could substitute for coherence.

Gameplay-wise, Star Fetchers is an action-adventure with light RPG elements, but the core interaction sounds awkward rather than exciting. The player controls the main character’s sword with the mouse, slicing enemies in precise cuts during aggressive, adrenaline-fueled battles. In theory that could create tense combat, but the description gives little reason to expect depth beyond repeated slashing. You also wander through a decadent world, fight other gangs, help out random weirdos, and uncover the horrible secrets of the Gray Zone, a so-called demilitarized district. That’s a lot of grimy flavor text, but not much indication of meaningful systems or satisfying progression.

Overall, Star Fetchers comes across less like a polished adventure and more like a loud, cluttered pitch for chaos. It promises bizarre scenarios and high-paced action, but the emphasis on ugliness, violence, and shock-value mood makes it feel self-indulgent rather than compelling.
