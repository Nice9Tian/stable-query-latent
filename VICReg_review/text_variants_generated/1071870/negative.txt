*Biped* presents itself as a charming cooperative adventure, but its appeal rests almost entirely on a gimmick: controlling two little robots, Aku and Sila, by separately moving their legs with two sticks or the left and right mouse buttons. In theory, this “innovative and simple” control scheme opens the door to walking, sliding, operating machinery, and even cutting wood. In practice, it sounds less like a refined system and more like a deliberately awkward setup designed to make every basic action feel like a coordination exercise.

The game centers on solo play or two-player co-op, where the goal is to communicate and coordinate through a series of challenges. That premise may sound wholesome, but it also suggests that progress depends heavily on the player’s ability to tolerate constant friction and keep synchronizing movements that would be trivial in a better-designed game. Instead of offering smooth platforming, *Biped* seems intent on turning every step into a test of patience and synchronization.

Its environments are described as beautiful and mysterious, stretching across forests, valleys, waterfalls, icy mountains, and paths leading to planetary beacons. Yet the emphasis on exploration does little to disguise how generic this structure appears. The game promises secrets and puzzling routes, but the information provided does not indicate anything especially deep or original beyond moving through themed locations.

There is also a cosmetic layer: players can collect treasures and buy goofy hats, then dress up their biped robots however they like. Matching your partner’s look is presented as a feature, though it feels more like a minor distraction than meaningful customization. Likewise, the level creation support through Steam Workshop offers some replay value, but it mainly serves as an acknowledgement that the base content may not be enough for long-term engagement.

Overall, *Biped* seems built around a quirky control idea, cooperative coordination, and light customization, but it also comes across as mechanically contrived and limited in ambition. What might be sold as a fun bonding journey instead sounds like a clumsy test of patience wrapped in a cheerful exterior.
