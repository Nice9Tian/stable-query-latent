Winkeltje: The Little Shop is an old-world fantasy shop management game in which you run your own store and shape it into a thriving business. As the shopkeeper, you handle buying, selling, inventory, and profit-making while trying to satisfy customer demand and make smart trading decisions. Goods can be purchased from traders or delivered for a fee, and the game emphasizes buying low and selling high across a wide range of products, including bulk goods, resources, vegetables, food, clothes, tools, and alchemical elixirs.

The game lets you manage your shop at your own pace, including choosing when to open for the day’s trading. It also includes a strong decorating and layout-building component, allowing you to place furniture and displays freely. You can furnish the shop with tables, display shelves, counters, storage pallets, and crafting stations, then add decorations to make the store more attractive to customers. The controls are designed to make furniture arrangement straightforward.

As your business grows, you can expand the shop by designing its floor plan and deciding where to place doors, windows, and archways. Wall and floor tiles can be combined freely to customize the store’s appearance and layout. The game also includes crafting systems, where resources can be turned into profitable items at dedicated stations such as tailor’s desks, forges, and alchemy tables. By gaining experience, you can craft higher-quality items that sell for more.

Food production is another part of the shop’s economy. You can grow crops in a vegetable garden by buying seeds, planting them, watering them, and harvesting produce such as artichokes, cauliflowers, and potatoes for sale in the shop. However, crops can wither and die if they are not properly cared for.

Winkeltje: The Little Shop also introduces changing seasonal customer needs and random events that can alter supply and demand, creating new challenges and opportunities for profit. When the workload becomes too much, you can hire employees by reviewing applications, choosing who to bring on, and firing staff if needed. Employee skill and salary management are part of the system as well, since raising worker skill and employer skill can help attract better talent and keep staff productive and satisfied.
