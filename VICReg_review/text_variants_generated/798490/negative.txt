Othercide presents itself as a grim tactical RPG, but its appeal is tied almost entirely to relentless suffering, repetition, and sacrifice. Humanity’s last hope is reduced to the Daughters, echoes of a legendary warrior, thrown into battle against nightmarish creatures born from humanity’s own crimes. The premise is bleak, and the game leans hard into that misery.

At its core, the game is about leading an army of Daughters through intricate turn-based combat. Players are expected to plan ahead, chain abilities together, and use counters to outsmart enemies. The much-touted Dynamic Timeline System promises “endless combat possibilities,” but in practice it is just another layer of complexity stacked on top of already punishing fights. The game constantly frames combat as a spectacle, yet it is really about making hard choices under pressure and watching those choices go wrong.

The most defining mechanic is sacrifice. You are explicitly encouraged to make the toughest decision and sacrifice one Daughter to heal another, with survivors becoming stronger for the next battle. Rather than creating meaningful emotional depth, this only reinforces the game’s cold, utilitarian approach to progression. Your army is treated as something to be managed, broken apart, and rebuilt after every failure.

Boss battles are presented as epic confrontations against the sources of Suffering, but the description suggests a world built on despair more than variety or player satisfaction. The narrative is described as deep and complex, though the page offers little beyond the usual promise of dread, death, and return. Even failure is not allowed to feel final; you simply come back with lost powers and face another nightmare.

Overall, Othercide seems less interested in providing comfort or clarity than in forcing players through a harsh loop of combat, loss, and recovery. It is an exhausting tactical experience wrapped in dark imagery and grand language, but one that appears to define itself more by punishment than by reward.
