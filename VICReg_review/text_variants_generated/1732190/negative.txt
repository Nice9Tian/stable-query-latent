FATAL FRAME / PROJECT ZERO: Maiden of Black Water is a rather tired attempt to revive a horror-adventure series by leaning on its anniversary rather than offering anything truly compelling. Its premise centers on Mt. Hikami, a spiritually revered mountain tied to water worship, gruesome incidents, and mysterious phenomena, but the story’s promise of dread is undercut by a structure that simply splits the experience into separate missions led by Yuri Kozukata, Miu Hinasaki, and Ren Hojo. Instead of building sustained tension, it keeps shuffling perspectives as it sends each protagonist deeper into the same gloomy mountain and its recycled secrets.

The core gameplay also feels limited by design. Players rely on the Camera Obscura, a special camera used to repel vengeful ghosts and seal away their power, which sounds distinctive but quickly becomes the obvious answer to nearly every problem. Shadow Reading is supposed to add intrigue by letting the protagonists sense lingering memories of missing people and guide them along hidden paths, but this mostly serves as another way to funnel the player into hostile encounters. The game’s combat and exploration are tied tightly to this single camera gimmick, and even utility functions like revealing hidden things and recovering lost items are wrapped into the same mechanic, making the whole system feel repetitive rather than versatile.

The supernatural encounters are described as malicious spirits trying to drag the characters into the world of the dead, and after defeating them the Glance ability lets players see their final moments. That may sound morbidly atmospheric, but it is really just another layer of the same formula: confront ghost, capture ghost, inspect ghost, move on. The result is less a haunting experience than a mechanical checklist of spectral chores.

The so-called new elements do not do much to improve matters. Improved screen resolution is hardly a meaningful creative addition, while the new outfits and accessories feel like cosmetic padding. Snap Mode, which lets players freely place characters and spirits for creative shots, seems especially out of place in a horror game that should be trying to maintain tension instead of encouraging staged photos. An updated Ghost List and updated controls are also included, but these read more like maintenance notes than exciting upgrades.

Even the post-game content feels oddly disconnected. After completing the main story, players can unlock special missions starring Ayane from the NINJA GAIDEN series. In Ayane Chapter, she cannot use the Camera Obscura at all, instead relying on ninja abilities, stealth, and hiding from ghosts under cover of night. While this does introduce a different style of play, it also highlights how rigid and one-note the main game is, since it takes a completely separate character and approach to create something fresh.

As a digital deluxe package, the game bundles a 20th Anniversary Digital Artbook with artwork and BGM tracks from past entries, plus a Past Protagonists Costume Set featuring outfits from earlier FATAL FRAME / PROJECT ZERO titles. These extras are clearly aimed at fans of the series rather than anyone looking for a strong standalone horror experience. Overall, Maiden of Black Water arrives on Steam with some anniversary trappings and a few superficial additions, but its reliance on repetitive camera mechanics and recycled series nostalgia makes it feel more like a modest relic than a truly memorable horror game.
