PGA TOUR 2K23 tries to sell itself as a swagger-filled golf game, but underneath the branding it is still very much a sports package built around licensed names, cosmetic extras, and a long list of modes that sound more substantial than they may feel. The various editions pile on bonus packs, virtual currency, and themed gear, while the core game promises a conventional PGA TOUR career mode and a spread of multiplayer options.

The Tiger Woods Edition is especially loaded with extras: a playable Michael Jordan bonus pack, 1,300 VC, a golden club set, Tiger Woods-themed apparel and equipment, and a Tiger Woods Signature Sunday Pack with specific clothing and branded golf balls. The Deluxe Edition includes most of the same incentive structure, though with fewer Tiger-specific bonuses. Even the store page’s own fine print reveals some of the cosmetic limitations, such as certain Tiger Woods Signature Sunday Pack items not being usable with female MyPLAYERs.

At its center, the game offers an “authentic” PGA TOUR MyCAREER mode where players build a MyPLAYER, compete against other pros, and attempt to become the next FedExCup Champion. The pitch is familiar and safe, and it leans heavily on progression and rivalry rather than anything especially bold or surprising. PGA TOUR pros and licensed courses are a major selling point, with names like Tiger Woods included and locations such as East Lake Golf Club, St. George’s Golf and Country Club, TPC Scottsdale, and TPC Sawgrass listed as part of the package.

The game also advertises its Course Designer as a way to create custom fantasy courses with thousands of objects, plus cross-platform sharing for online and local play. This sounds ambitious, though it is still the kind of user-generated feature that often exists to extend replay value rather than to transform the experience. Topgolf is another addition, framed as a casual 1-4 player mode for both solo and group play, but it reads more like a side attraction than a core identity shift.

Progression is tied to new Skills and Archetypes, while licensed gear from brands like adidas, Callaway, Titleist, and Malbon is meant to add style to the grind. There are also multiple control and accessibility options, including a new 3-Click swing system, the traditional analog Swing Stick, real-time tutorials, tips, shot suggestions, the TrueShot System, and Lie Management. These features suggest an effort to be approachable, though they also underline how much the game relies on systems and assistance to keep players engaged.

Online Societies let players manage tournaments, seasons, entry rules, handicaps, and event settings, which gives the game some structural flexibility. Multiplayer support includes Alt-Shot, Stroke Play, Skins, and 4-Player Scramble, along with the return of Divot Derby, a single-elimination shoot-out mode. Overall, PGA TOUR 2K23 appears to be a heavily licensed, feature-stacked golf title that leans on familiar modes, cosmetic rewards, and brand name appeal more than on any truly fresh idea.
