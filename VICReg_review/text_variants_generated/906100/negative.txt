Observation presents itself as a sci-fi thriller, but its premise is more interesting than its execution. The game follows Dr. Emma Fisher and the fate of her mission through the perspective of the station’s artificial intelligence, S.A.M., with players taking control of the station itself rather than a human character. That idea sounds distinctive at first, but it is also the main thing the game has going for it.

Gameplay revolves around operating the station’s control systems, cameras, and tools to help Emma investigate what is happening to the station, the missing crew, and S.A.M. himself. In practice, this means the player spends much of the experience acting as a remote interface, piecing together events through observation and limited interaction. The game blends narrative adventure, puzzles, exploration, and cosmic existential horror, though those elements seem designed more to support the concept than to deliver anything especially memorable or deep.

The story is centered on uncovering the true nature of the crew, the station, and the mysterious hexagon on Saturn that is always present in the background. While this setup promises mystery, it also leans heavily on vague sci-fi intrigue. Even the tagline, “You’re not on the station, you are the station,” feels more like a pitch than a satisfying promise of gameplay.

Observation is ultimately a game built around an unusual point of view and a moody sci-fi premise, but it appears to depend almost entirely on atmosphere and concept. For players looking for a more substantial or varied experience, its limited mechanics and abstract narrative may feel thin despite the ambition behind it.
