Songs of Syx is a fantasy city-builder and grand strategy game set in a low-fantasy world, where players begin with a small colony and gradually develop it into a sprawling metropolis and empire. The game emphasizes complex systems, large-scale management, and consequences that can ripple from minor disruptions to the collapse of kingdoms. It is described as easy to learn but difficult to master, with visuals designed to stimulate the player’s imagination rather than aim for realism.

The game world is presented as a living and changing setting populated by different races, cultures, gods, and strange creatures. Day and night cycles, weather, climate shifts, and migrations of animals and people all affect the world. Events such as droughts, pestilence, pirates, bandits, and new discoveries can alter the rise and fall of kingdoms whether or not the player intervenes. Each playthrough generates content, background stories, and easter eggs uniquely, while still following an overarching lore.

City-building in Songs of Syx is centered on survival, expansion, and managing a growing population. Players must provide food, clothing, and other necessities, starting from the first winter by hunting, foraging, and farming. Settlements can grow to tens of thousands of individuals, and management gradually shifts from focusing on single people to overseeing groups defined by race, social standing, religion, and trade. The population is made up of individuals with their own personalities, skills, flaws, preferences, friends, and traits. Rather than direct control, the player gives plans that the people carry out intelligently. Good treatment encourages immigration, while mistreatment can lead to inefficiency or rebellion.

A major part of the game is social and political management. Different groups do not necessarily like each other, and the player can choose to encourage discrimination and privileges or instead build a more tolerant and diverse society. Nobles can be appointed or imported from other kingdoms to manage different aspects of the realm, improving production and standing, but they may become untrustworthy, attempt to usurp power, or join enemies. Slaves are also part of the system, serving in the settlement while requiring restraint to prevent revolt.

As the settlement expands, the game becomes a broader grand strategy experience. A capital must be supported by villages, towns, lumber camps, castles, and ports across the kingdom. Diplomacy and cultural relations matter, since other kingdoms and despots may seek alliances or territorial expansion. Trade is global and dynamic, with no fixed prices and no currency to mint; values are driven by supply and demand, meaning wealth can change quickly depending on market conditions.

Combat is another major feature. Songs of Syx supports massive tactical battles with up to 20,000 individual units and clashes of up to 40,000 units. Players can gather allies, vassals, mercenaries, and heroes to strengthen their forces. Battles are described as realistic, with low kill rates and fragile morale, where tactics such as encirclement and reputation can determine the outcome. Warfare takes place mostly outside cities and on open fields as the game progresses, and defeat can be devastating, forcing the player to recover with manpower, funds, and diplomacy.

The setting includes a detailed backstory about a former empire ruled under the Gods of Order, its destruction after the God of Chaos was released, and the catastrophic wars, plague, and magical devastation that followed. After the gods’ avatars were slain and the God of Chaos was eventually assassinated, an age of ice covered the world. With the ice now receded, the world is said to need new leadership.

Players can choose from different factions, including the Dondorians, master miners who seek Dyhrlium and dream of rebuilding their capital at Mt. Cerebus; Men, who are fragile but inventive and capable of uniting their fractured kingdoms; and the Kantors, a reclusive people from the frozen north who may become valuable allies if attracted to a city. The game’s development is still ongoing, with the base game described as partially complete and unpolished, and the developers state that it will remain in Early Access for a long time while they continue expanding and refining it.
