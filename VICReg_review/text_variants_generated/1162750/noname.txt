This fantasy city-builder places the player in charge of a tiny colony and tasks them with turning it into a sprawling metropolis and, eventually, an empire. The experience is built around layered systems rather than simple micromanagement: it is easy to approach, but difficult to fully master, and even small disruptions can trigger major consequences. The world is presented as a low-fantasy setting with stylized visuals intended to support the player’s imagination rather than strict realism.

The game world is designed to feel alive and dynamic. Different races, cultures, gods, and strange creatures inhabit it, while day and night cycles, changing climates, weather shifts, and the movement of people and animals all affect the larger political and economic landscape. Events such as droughts, disease, pirates, bandits, and discoveries can cause kingdoms to rise or fall, and each playthrough is generated with unique content, background details, and hidden elements while still following a broader narrative framework.

At its core, the game focuses on settlement growth and administration on a very large scale. Players begin by securing the basics needed to survive the first winter, including food production, clothing, hunting, foraging, and farming. The population is made up of individual people, each with their own personalities, preferences, relationships, traits, and goals. They are not controlled directly; instead, the player sets plans and the inhabitants carry them out intelligently. Treating them well can attract more immigrants, while mistreatment can reduce efficiency or even provoke rebellion.

As the settlement expands, the game shifts from individual management to group-based society building. Populations are organized by combinations of race, social standing, religion, and profession, and different groups may dislike or distrust one another. The player can either encourage division through discrimination and privilege, or build a more diverse and tolerant society that makes use of varied racial strengths.

Expansion also brings broader political responsibilities. A growing capital depends on supporting settlements such as villages, towns, lumber camps, fortresses, and ports throughout the realm. Other rulers may seek alliances or threaten expansion, so diplomacy and cultural positioning matter. Trade is fully systemic, with no fixed prices and no currency to mint; value rises and falls according to supply and demand, making wealth unstable if not carefully managed.

Military conflict is another major pillar. Players can gather forces from allies and vassals, recruit mercenaries and heroes, and command battles involving tens of thousands of units. Combat emphasizes tactics, morale, encirclement, and attrition, with relatively low kill rates and the possibility of entire armies breaking and fleeing. Battles can decide the fate of kingdoms, and losing a major engagement can be disastrous, often requiring costly peace terms or rapid rebuilding.

Social hierarchy and internal governance are also significant. Slaves can be used for labor, while nobles can be appointed or imported to oversee different aspects of the realm, improve productivity, and strengthen political standing. However, these powerful figures must be managed carefully, since they may become ambitious, defect to rivals, or attempt to seize power.

The setting includes a long historical backstory centered on a shattered world once unified under a mighty empire and later devastated by divine conflict, plague, magical warfare, and a long age of ice. That frozen era has now ended, leaving the world open for renewed leadership. Players can choose among different peoples with distinct strengths and histories, including sturdy mountain dwellers, fragile but inventive mortals, and secluded northern survivors whose value can be immense if they can be persuaded to join a settlement.

Overall, the game is presented as a long-term early-access project with many planned systems still being refined. The foundation already includes a substantial portion of the intended features, but the developers emphasize that the experience is still unfinished and will remain in development for a long time.
