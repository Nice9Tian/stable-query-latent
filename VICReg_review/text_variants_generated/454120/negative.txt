Starbase presents itself as an ambitious space MMO, but the store page makes it hard to ignore how unfinished it still is. It launches in Early Access in a clear alpha stage, and that weakness hangs over everything the game tries to promise. The core systems are apparently in place, but the page repeatedly leans on potential, scale, and future updates rather than a truly polished experience.

At its center, Starbase is about building and designing spaceships and stations, then using them for exploration, resource gathering, crafting, trading, and combat. Players can create ships, stations, and devices down to the tiniest detail, with a single bolt as the smallest building unit. In theory, this allows for elaborate constructions, including automated factories, electricity networks, and data networks. The game also encourages players to sell blueprints, use programming chips, and invent clever ship layouts. Yet all of this emphasis on complexity also makes the game sound intimidating and excessively technical, especially for something still described as an alpha.

The universe is presented as a single persistent online space with thousands of players, but the store page frames much of the experience around what players might eventually accomplish rather than what is stable now. You can mine asteroids, haul cargo, trade resources, group up with others, form factions, and build massive stations. There are also social hubs, wrecks, asteroid fields, and unknown dangers to explore. However, the constant focus on cooperation, systems mastery, and player ingenuity suggests a game that depends heavily on community coordination and self-directed goals, which may feel more like work than play to many players.

Combat is another major pillar, combining FPS action with spaceship battles in a fully destructible environment. The page stresses that everything can be destroyed or disassembled with the right tools and weaponry, and that the physics simulation creates creative possibilities. But it also admits that poor design choices can literally break ships in half, and the mention of station destruction being added later undercuts confidence in the current state of the game. The result is a game that sounds brittle as often as it sounds impressive.

Starbase also relies on a player-driven economy where you can buy, sell, trade, salvage, and even steal materials, parts, weapons, tools, ammo, and designs. That system may sound dynamic, but it also implies a world where players must constantly grind, optimize, and compete for resources. Safe zones are available around starting stations, which feels like a necessary concession in a universe otherwise full of pirates and hazards. Even here, the game’s structure seems to be built around protecting beginners from the chaos its own design creates.

The technical ambitions are obvious, with Frozenbyte’s custom engine built specifically for the game’s voxel and vertex-based simulation. The page insists this architecture can handle damage, building, player counts, and the universe’s scale. But the long list of intended systems, combined with repeated references to future expansion across moons, planets, and star systems, makes Starbase feel like a project still trying to justify itself. It may have a foundation, but the store page does little to hide that it is still a rough, unfinished, and demanding alpha built on grand promises.
