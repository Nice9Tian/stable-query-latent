*Brotato* sets up a thin, absurd premise and then stretches it across a survival loop that quickly becomes repetitive. A spaceship from Potato World crashes on an alien planet, leaving Brotato as the only survivor and the only potato able to wield 6 weapons at once. The goal is simply to last until rescue arrives, but the game reduces that idea to wave after wave of survival against hostile aliens.

The combat is built around auto-firing weapons by default, with manual aiming available if desired, which makes the action feel more like a constant screen-filling grind than a tactical challenge. Matches are fast, usually under 30 minutes, but that brevity does little to hide how much of the experience is just the same loop repeated: survive waves lasting 20 to 90 seconds, kill as many aliens as possible, collect materials, gain experience, and then shop between waves. It is a blunt progression system that leans heavily on repetition rather than depth.

There is some variety on paper, with dozens of characters that change runs in different ways, including one-handed, crazy, lucky, mage, and others. The game also offers hundreds of items and weapons, from flamethrowers and SMGs to rocket launchers and even sticks and stones. But this abundance feels more like clutter than meaningful design, since the core activity remains the same regardless of loadout. Local co-op for up to 4 players is included, though it does not change the underlying formula much.

Even the accessibility options, which let players tweak enemy health, damage, and speed, underline how much the game depends on raw numbers instead of engaging systems. *Brotato* is a lightweight survival shooter with plenty of content on the surface, but beneath that it is a noisy, repetitive, and ultimately shallow loop of shooting, shopping, and surviving waves until boredom or rescue comes first.
