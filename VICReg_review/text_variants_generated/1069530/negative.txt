Narita Boy is presented as a retro-flavored action-adventure that tries very hard to sell itself as an epic 80s power fantasy, but the premise is mostly style over substance. The story centers on The Creator, who made the Narita One console and its hit game Narita Boy, only for the digital world to become threatened after Him returns and erases The Creator’s memories. Motherboard activates the Narita Boy protocol, the Stallions advance, and the Digital Kingdom supposedly needs a hero.

In practice, the game is built around exploring the Digital Kingdom in all directions while uncovering the mysteries of the Techno-sword and fighting corrupted Stallions. The page promises a “radical action-adventure” where you play as a legendary pixel hero reduced to an echo inside this digital world. Combat revolves around the Techno-sword, the only weapon said to be capable of defeating the threat, and the game also emphasizes boss encounters against creatures like Crab, DragonBot, and Black Rainbow.

The game leans heavily on presentation, boasting hand-drawn animations meant to recreate a retro era and a soundtrack filled with synth-heavy beats. It clearly wants to impress with visual flair and nostalgic atmosphere, but that emphasis on style is also its main identity. The store page makes a point of calling it a “never-ending story,” even though it awkwardly adds that there is, in fact, an end.

Overall, Narita Boy is a game that seems far more interested in loudly celebrating its own aesthetic than in offering anything especially original. Its world, enemies, and weapons are framed with exaggerated retro bravado, but underneath the neon rhetoric it is still just an action-adventure about moving through a broken digital realm, fighting bosses, and saving a world that is constantly insisting on how cool it is.
