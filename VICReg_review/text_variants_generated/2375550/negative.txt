**Like a Dragon Gaiden: The Man Who Erased His Name** is a deliberately narrow spin-off that leans heavily on familiar Yakuza formulae without seeming to add much of its own. It follows Kazuma Kiryu after he fakes his death and abandons his name to protect his family, only to be dragged back into conflict by a mysterious figure determined to flush him out of hiding. Under the codename “Joryu,” he is pushed through a story-driven action campaign set across vibrant locations, but the premise feels more like a reuse of old elements than a bold new chapter.

The combat centers on two switching fighting styles, and while that sounds flexible, the game’s appeal remains rooted in straightforward brawling. The Yakuza style is built around aggressive melee attacks and raw strength, while the Agent style emphasizes speed, precision, and gadgets such as electrified bind wires that can stun enemies and send them flying. The game encourages adapting between the two styles, yet the overall impression is still one of repetitive crowd combat rather than anything especially refined.

Outside the main fights, the game stuffs in a range of side activities, from the secret Battle Arena to karaoke, a live-action cabaret club, and pocket circuit racing. Akame also offers sub-missions that gradually pull players deeper into the game’s version of Sotenbori and Yokohama. There is clearly a lot packed into the experience, but it reads less like meaningful variety and more like a checklist of distractions surrounding the central beat-’em-up structure.

The Deluxe Edition adds the Legendary Fighter Pack, which lets players enter the Coliseum and use or fight alongside Goro Majima, Taiga Saejima, and Daigo Dojima. The page also includes a Like a Dragon: Infinite Wealth Special Trial Version that unlocks after completing the game. Even with these extras, the package comes across as an aggressively familiar entry that relies on franchise loyalty more than innovation.
