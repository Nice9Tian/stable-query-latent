NieR Replicant™ ver.1.22474487139... is presented as a modernized re-release of the Japan-only NieR Replicant, but the page content mostly leans on praise and grand promises rather than anything truly substantial. It frames itself as the prequel to NieR:Automata, yet the appeal is clearly built on inherited reputation instead of a fresh identity.

The story follows a kind young man from a remote village who sets out with Grimoire Weiss, a talking tome, to save his sister Yonah after she falls ill with the Black Scrawl. The game positions this as a brother’s perspective on the NieR Replicant story, set in a distant future where humanity is near extinction, strange beasts roam the world, and a “thousand-year lie” supposedly underpins everything. It all sounds dramatic, but the page itself relies heavily on vague, ominous language to sell the narrative.

Gameplay is described in broad strokes as a mix of swordplay and magic, with combat that is “visceral” and “uniquely customizable.” The main mechanical hook mentioned is the Words system, which lets players customize character magic, skills, and weapons. Beyond that, the page offers little concrete detail, making the combat sound more impressive in theory than it is explained in practice.

The page also emphasizes presentation upgrades, including “masterfully revived visuals” and a newly recorded soundtrack by Keiichi Okabe. An “all-star team” is highlighted, with YOKO TARO, Keiichi Okabe, and Yosuke Saito returning, but this is presented more as a marketing badge than evidence of anything especially new or daring.

There is also free DLC called 4 YoRHa, which includes 4 costumes and 4 weapons from NieR:Automata. However, the page admits that due to the story, this content may not be playable during some scenes, and the full game plus the latest patch are required. That small bonus feels restricted and secondary rather than meaningful.

Overall, the page sells NieR Replicant™ ver.1.22474487139... as a dark, emotional, upgraded prequel with customizable combat and a celebrated soundtrack, but the description remains frustratingly abstract. It seems to ask for admiration based on atmosphere, legacy, and names attached, while revealing very little about why the experience would feel genuinely compelling.
