Between the Stars is a space action game wrapped in RPG progression and real-time combat, but its ambitions often sound more exciting than the experience likely feels. You sit in command of an interstellar cruiser and are tasked with protecting civilization from the Children of the Sun, a faction spreading across border planets. The premise sets up a galactic conflict, yet most of the gameplay revolves around the usual loop of jumping between sectors, surviving random events, and trying to keep your ship functional long enough to continue.

The game pushes a mix of combat, management, and decision-making. You fight real-time space battles, trigger ship abilities at the right moment, and upgrade equipment to stay alive. There is also a captain system, crew management, ship unlocking and customization, and a procedural universe filled with events, stations, and planets. On paper, this sounds broad, but it also reads like a checklist of familiar roguelite features rather than a truly distinctive identity.

Its reliance on text-based events and branching decisions is one of its main hooks, though even this is framed in a fairly punishing way. Choices can affect the ship, crew, and even the wider playthrough, but that also means the game leans heavily on luck and consequence-heavy outcomes. You can hire and level up crew, craft and salvage weapons, and develop your captain with attributes shaped by decisions, yet these systems seem designed more to layer complexity than to create depth.

Between the Stars also offers a standard difficulty mode and a permadeath option for those who want extra punishment. That fits the overall tone of a game that appears to value endurance and attrition over smooth pacing. It promises a galaxy full of danger, but the structure is built around repetitive survival, procedural events, and managing losses rather than delivering a consistently compelling adventure.
