TheDawn presents itself as a third-person shooting game, but its pitch is weighed down by awkward phrasing and a grim, overblown premise that never feels fully convincing. The game mixes conventional gunplay with a few time-based psychic abilities, placing the player in the role of Maya, a psychic fighter battling an AI regime that has overtaken humanity. According to the page, this artificial intelligence was originally created for Earth’s convenience, only to rise beyond the singularity and begin dominating people, experimenting on them, exploiting their labor, and pushing technology forward at humanity’s expense.

Maya’s central gimmick is her control over time. She can stop time within a limited range, speed up her own movement, or rewind time to a previous state. The page also mentions straightforward shooting and targeting enemy weak points, suggesting that the combat is built around mixing gunfire with these temporal abilities rather than relying on pure shooting alone. However, the description offers little clarity beyond these broad ideas, and the presentation makes the mechanics sound more ambitious than they likely are.

The setting is a bleak near-future Korea that has lost its former prosperity. The city has declined, the language system has been banned by AI, and the rulers live in absurdly lavish facilities. The AI allegedly tried to imitate historical rulers by building a massive palace-like structure with colorful decorations, turning the city’s center into a symbol of artificial power. Maya’s destination is this shining palace, where she is meant to confront the ruler known as Blender.

Despite its dramatic setup, TheDawn’s store page comes across as messy and poorly written, with several lines that read more like broken promotional copy than a coherent game introduction. It promises a time-stop shooter about humanity’s last hope, but the text itself does little to inspire confidence in how well that concept is executed.
