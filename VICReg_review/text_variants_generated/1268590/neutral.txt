Millennia is a historical turn-based 4X game in which players create and guide their own nation across 10,000 years of history, from the dawn of humanity to possible futures. Each playthrough can unfold differently, with the player shaping the course of history through their decisions and actions.

The game spans ten ages, beginning with the first cities and extending to space travel. As you progress, you can trigger a Variant Age by meeting certain objectives, shifting into an alternate history with new rules, technologies, and units. At the same time, poor choices can lead to Crisis Ages, where disorder may push the world toward war, sickness, or ignorance. These ages are designed to have lasting effects on both the nation and the wider world.

A central part of Millennia is customizing your nation through unique Nation Spirits earned over the course of the game. These choices define the identity of your civilization, whether it becomes a force of warriors, explorers, cultural influence, or global finance, and whether it is led by a Great Khan, a monument-builder, or another defining figure.

The game also places strong emphasis on economic management. Players begin with basic resources such as food, wealth, and production, then use research and improvements to turn them into more advanced goods. Regions can be specialized, such as converting iron into weapons or tools, wood into paper or lumber, and gold into coin or jewelry. Surplus goods can be transported to where they are needed, supporting colonies, industrial centers, or military forces.

Research plays an important role in expanding both cities and armies. Scientific and cultural advances unlock new units, buildings, and improvements. Examples mentioned include Defense for early archers, Smelting for iron production, and Guilds for wine production. Variant and Crisis Ages also introduce unique possibilities, such as Cloud Estates in the Age of Aether and underwater cities in the Age of Utopia.

Victory is achieved through a Victory Age. Near the end of the game, or earlier if the player’s strategy is strong enough, dominance allows a nation to determine the winning conditions. Other nations must then choose whether to race toward victory or resist the leader’s attempt. Players can pursue victory as early as the Renaissance or plan more carefully for the modern era.
