In this historical turn-based 4X strategy game, players create their own nation and guide it across 10,000 years of history, from humanity’s earliest beginnings to possible future eras. Each playthrough can unfold differently, with the course of history shaped by the player’s decisions. The experience centers on leading a civilization through crises, discoveries, and major turning points while building a society that can endure across the ages.

The game spans ten distinct ages, beginning with the first cities and eventually reaching space travel. Certain objectives can trigger a shift into a special alternate-history era, where new rules, technologies, and units change the direction of play. However, there are also crisis eras, where disorder can push the world toward futures marked by war, sickness, or ignorance. These periods can also be turned to advantage by a capable ruler, making age progression a major strategic layer.

Nation identity is highly customizable through a series of unique spirit choices gathered over the course of a campaign. These shape the nation’s character, whether it becomes focused on warfare, exploration, cultural influence, monumental building, or financial dominance. This system allows different historical themes to emerge depending on how the player wants their civilization to be remembered.

Economic management is a central part of the experience. Players begin with basic resources such as food, wealth, and production, then use research and improvements to transform those raw materials into more advanced goods. Regions can be specialized, turning materials like iron, wood, and gold into useful outputs such as weapons, tools, paper, lumber, coin, or jewelry. Surplus resources can also be transported to where they are needed, supporting distant colonies or reinforcing military production near the front lines.

Research drives both scientific and cultural progress, unlocking new units, buildings, and improvements that strengthen cities and armies. Early examples include defensive training, metal production, and specialized production methods for goods like wine. The alternate-history and crisis eras expand the technology and development landscape further, introducing unusual possibilities such as cloud-based estates or underwater cities.

Victory is handled through a special late-game phase in which dominance can allow one nation to set the terms of success. Other powers must then decide whether to race for victory before the leader or oppose that nation directly. This creates a final strategic struggle where players may aim for an earlier triumph in one era or carefully prepare for a more deliberate path to victory in a modern setting.
