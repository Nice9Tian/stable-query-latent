DON'T SCREAM is a shallow Unreal Engine 5 horror experience that leans heavily on a single gimmick: if you scream, gasp, or even whimper into your microphone, the game restarts. Marketed as a 1990s camcorder-style found-footage scarefest, it asks you to survive one night in a forest without screaming or dying, while time only moves when you do. That constraint sounds interesting at first, but in practice it mainly serves to slow everything down and force the player to creep through a hostile environment where the scares are deliberately unpredictable.

The game promises dozens of dynamic scares, so each run is supposed to feel different, but the structure underneath remains fairly thin. You are told to collect batteries to keep the recording going, because running out means game over. You also have to avoid hunters lurking in the forest, explore a large haunted area, and look for relics scattered around as fragments of some vague past. The result is a checklist of survival tasks rather than a deep horror system.

The biggest problem is that the whole experience is built around a single microphone requirement and a restart punishment that can feel more annoying than frightening. Instead of creating tension through strong design, it relies on startling the player and hoping the novelty carries the rest. The “scare your friends and family” pitch says a lot about its priorities: it seems more interested in cheap reactions than sustained horror.
