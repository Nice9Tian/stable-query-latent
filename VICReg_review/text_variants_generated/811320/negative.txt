**Jupiter Hell** is a brutally old-school sci-fi roguelike that seems determined to punish nostalgia rather than celebrate it. It proudly rejects hand-holding, and its “you act, enemies act” turn-based structure means every mistake is immediately your own. Death is permanent, so each run can end in a wipe with no safety net. The game leans hard into a grim 90s-flavored cosmic-hell aesthetic, with CRT-style presentation and a world packed with demonic and alien enemies.

Combat revolves around a small arsenal of familiar weapons like shotguns, chainguns, railguns, and even a chainsaw, but there is no sign that firepower alone will spare you from the game’s hostility. Procedurally generated levels ensure that each run changes, while perks and character builds are meant to provide some sense of progression between doomed attempts. Even that progress feels deliberately oppressive, since surviving only unlocks harder challenges that are explicitly designed to make suffering worse, not better.

The game comes from ChaosForge, the team behind *Doom, the Roguelike* (DRL), and *Jupiter Hell* positions itself as a spiritual successor to that project. That pedigree may appeal to players who enjoy punishing, turn-based roguelikes with a classic top-down feel. But for everyone else, it sounds less like an invitation and more like a warning: this is a game that wants to kill you, over and over, while pretending that’s the fun part.
