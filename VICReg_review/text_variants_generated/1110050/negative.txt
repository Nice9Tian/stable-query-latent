Evan’s Remains is a mystery-thriller puzzle adventure built around the search for a missing boy named Evan, but it leans heavily on its own obscurity and deliberate pacing. The game mixes logic-based platform puzzles with a narrative structure inspired by Japanese graphic adventure games, yet the result sounds more like a slow drip of information than a compelling mystery. Progress is tightly gated: after every two to three puzzles, the story advances and a new mechanic appears. Rather than testing reflexes or skill, the puzzles depend on figuring out what to do, which may appeal to some players but can also make the experience feel needlessly opaque. As the difficulty rises, the game simply stacks previously introduced mechanics together, turning complexity into accumulation instead of genuine variety.

The story starts with Evan, a gifted boy who vanished years ago. Suddenly, a letter signed by him arrives, claiming he is on an uninhabited island in the Pacific Ocean and specifically asking for a girl named Dysis to come find him. The player controls Dysis, though the page offers very little about who she is. Her journey is meant to slowly answer questions and connect the scattered puzzle pieces, but the setup relies on withholding almost everything at the start. Instead of immediately creating intrigue, the narrative structure seems designed to stretch out basic revelations until the truth is eventually assembled piece by piece.
