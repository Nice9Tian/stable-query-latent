*The Exit 8* is a short walking simulator built around one painfully simple idea: you are trapped in an endless underground passageway and must keep staring at the environment for anything out of place. The game leans on Japanese underground corridors, liminal spaces, and backrooms-style unease, but it does so with very little beyond that premise. Its core rule is blunt and repetitive: if you spot an anomaly, turn back immediately; if you do not, keep going. That is the entire loop.

The problem is that this minimalism feels less like elegance and more like thin content stretched to fill a game. The experience is reportedly only 15 to 60 minutes long, which makes the whole structure feel even more barebones. There is no real complexity to the gameplay beyond observation and reversal, and the game itself practically admits that if you understand the anomaly rule, you can probably finish it without much trouble. The result is a compact but limited experience that depends almost entirely on one gimmick.

On the technical side, there are a few configurable options: graphics settings, mouse sensitivity, camera shake, camera acceleration, and motion blur. These are presented partly as comfort settings for players prone to motion sickness, which is sensible, but it also underlines how little else there is to customize. Even the options section feels more like damage control than meaningful depth.

*The Exit 8* is available only in Japanese and English, and video distribution is mentioned only in reference to the official KOTAKE CREATE website. Overall, it comes across as an extremely short, sparse walking simulator that asks the player to scrutinize a corridor over and over, offering atmosphere and a single rule rather than much in the way of substance.
