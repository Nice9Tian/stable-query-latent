*Forgive Me Father* is a grimly derivative FPS that leans hard on Lovecraftian horror and retro aesthetics, but the store page makes it sound more impressive than it probably feels in practice. You play as the last person with full senses, wandering through a hostile world in search of answers, relief, and a way to make sense of the madness around you. The premise is serviceable, if familiar: an occult nightmare filled with dread, confusion, and eternal evil, all wrapped in a comic book presentation that tries to look like hand-drawn panels brought to life.

Mechanically, the game promises a classic 90s-style shooter structure, complete with health kits, life points, non-reloading weapons, and powerups thrown into large-scale combat. It also adds a madness system that changes dynamically during gameplay. According to the description, this madness affects both the audiovisual presentation and the gameplay itself, and can even grant additional power. That sounds like a gimmick meant to disguise standard shooter design with a layer of instability. You also get active skills, and the player is told to choose them based on play style, while equipment evolution is left in the player’s hands. In theory, this creates some customization; in practice, it reads like a pile of loosely connected systems trying to compensate for the lack of a genuinely original core.

The game is presented in a retro horror comic book style, with 2D elements layered into a 3D environment and timelapse animations. The page insists that any screenshot could pass as a comic panel, which is a nice way of saying the visuals are doing most of the heavy lifting. The world is described as hand-made, full of hidden and hard-to-reach areas, and the levels are supposed to be varied and climactic. Enemies range from possessed inhabitants to monsters, forming a bestiary that is clearly built to evoke Lovecraft’s stories rather than develop anything of its own.

There are two playable heroes, a priest and a journalist, each with a unique development tree, which at least offers a minimal reason to replay the story from another angle. But even that sounds more like a standard branching choice than a meaningful twist. Overall, *Forgive Me Father* appears to be one more horror shooter that relies on atmosphere, comic-book styling, and Lovecraft branding to carry a formula that is otherwise all too familiar.
