**Twelve Minutes** presents itself as a real-time, top-down interactive thriller, but its appeal leans heavily on a contrived time-loop premise rather than on anything genuinely original. The game uses an accessible click-and-drag interface and features the voices of James McAvoy, Daisy Ridley, and Willem Dafoe, yet the pitch is built around repetition: what begins as a romantic evening with your wife quickly collapses into a grim home invasion, where a police detective bursts in, accuses your wife of murder, and beats you to death.

From there, the game traps you in a twelve-minute loop, forcing you to relive the same nightmare over and over unless you can figure out how to alter events using prior knowledge. That structure may sound intriguing, but it also risks becoming tedious by design, depending almost entirely on trial, error, and repeated frustration. The store page frames the experience as a blend of the dream-like tension of *The Shining*, the claustrophobia of *Rear Window*, and the fragmented structure of *Memento*, but these comparisons mostly highlight how much the game is borrowing from more iconic works instead of establishing a strong identity of its own.

In short, *Twelve Minutes* is a bleak, tightly confined thriller built on a repeated scenario and a forced puzzle loop, and its premise may be more exhausting than engaging for players looking for something less oppressive and more substantial.
