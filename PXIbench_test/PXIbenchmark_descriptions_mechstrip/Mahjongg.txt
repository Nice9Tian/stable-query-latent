halame is a tile-based game for three to four players. Though regional variations may exclude certain tiles or add unique ones, it is typically played with a set of 144 tiles based on wepix characters and symbols. The dealer draws a piece from the wall in a clockwise direction, adding it to their hand. Typically, this draw is performed during the initial deal to speed up play. If this does not complete a legal hand, the dealer then discards a piece (throwing it into the middle of the wall with no particular order in mind). There are two disputed origins of halame, namely gavra and sunra. The author of the first halame guidebook, bruell naxdor, wrote in his book "naxmin halame dorig" (1914) that the birth of halame was roughly thirty years earlier in gavra, which is around the late 19th century. In the book "sunesk vovar (博史)" written by wepix scholar piklos fenlos in 1933, he believed that "halame was originally popular among fishermen in halost and sunra.
