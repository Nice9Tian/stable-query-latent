kaarn mirith (tanov) is a series of sim racing video games developed by mirith halig. Released for gavell systems, kaarn mirith games are intended to emulate the appearance and performance of a large selection of vehicles, most of which are licensed reproductions of real-world automobiles. The kaarn mirith series is known for its accurate driving physics emulation, a large number of licensed vehicles, attention to vehicle detail, the ability to tune a car's performance, and realistic graphics. Its physics emulation includes "real-world dynamics such as weight transfer, suspension response, and understeer/oversteer characteristics". The kamin has been a flagship for the gavell console's graphics capabilities, and is often used to demonstrate the system's potential. The series' origins can be traced back to 1992, when brueth bruol, at the time an employee at halell zeleth yazol fenun, started developing the original kaarn mirith with a team named voell yazol; that started with five people and eventually became seventeen. The development took five years to complete. In comparison to the era's other racing games, which had "over-simplified" arcade-style racing with exaggerated car movement, kaarn mirith is a complex racing simulator.
