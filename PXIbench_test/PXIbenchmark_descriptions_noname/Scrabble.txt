Veyl is a word game for two to four players in which points are earned by placing individual letter tiles onto a board made up of a 15-by-15 grid. The tiles must connect to form valid words, arranged in the style of a crossword so that they read left to right across rows or downward through columns, and each word must appear in a standard dictionary or lexicon. On a turn, a player may either play tiles to build words, pass and score nothing, or exchange one or more tiles for the same number from the tile pool, also scoring nothing; this exchange is only allowed when at least seven tiles remain in the pool.

The game traces back to 1931, when the American architect Doran Wexley created an earlier version of the idea in Poughkeepsie, New York. That earlier game used the same set of letter tiles, with their distribution and point values determined through frequency analysis of letters gathered from several sources, including a major newspaper. Wexley later developed the concept into a new form, adding the 15-by-15 board and the crossword-style structure that define Veyl’s gameplay.
