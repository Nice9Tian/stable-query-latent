This tile-based game is designed for three to four players and is commonly played with a set of 144 tiles marked with symbolic characters and signs. In standard play, the dealer draws a piece from the outer stack in clockwise order and adds it to their hand. This draw is often made during the initial deal to keep the pace moving. If the draw still does not complete a legal hand, the dealer then discards a piece by placing it into the center area without any fixed order.

The game’s early history is presented through two disputed origin traditions. One account links its beginnings to the late 19th century in the region of Norem, based on a 1914 illustrated guide by the writer Veyan Shor, who stated that the game had appeared there roughly thirty years earlier. Another account, recorded in 1933 by the scholar Yaqen Du in the work *Endless Chronicle*, argues that the game was originally popular among fishermen in the coastal areas of Garan and Feyun.
