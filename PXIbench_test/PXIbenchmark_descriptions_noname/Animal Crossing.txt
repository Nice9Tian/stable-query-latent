This social simulation title was first released in 2001 for a Japanese 64-bit console, then later received an enhanced port for a home system. Its publisher described it as a “communication game,” emphasizing everyday interaction rather than fixed objectives. The experience is open-ended, allowing the player’s character to live a separate life with very little set plot or mandatory tasks. Players take on the role of a new resident arriving in a town and are free to shape their routine at their own pace.

The game was developed by an in-house studio with a relatively inexperienced team led by three creators who had regrouped after an earlier project in 1997. It was also presented at a major 2000 showcase event, where playable versions of four classic mini-games were included: “Blimbo Flight,” “Frost Climber,” “Twirl Twirl Land,” and “Ape Block.”
