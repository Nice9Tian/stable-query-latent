This independent first-person shooter, here referred to as **Veyron**, presents action through a stripped-down visual language: scarlet hostiles, dark implements, and a sparse white-and-gray setting. The player advances through minimalist spaces and must eliminate attackers before being killed themselves. Combat is built around scarcity and improvisation, since picked-up implements have limited uses and break easily, pushing the player to seize new gear from defeated enemies or rely on close-range takedowns.

A single hit from an enemy projectile is fatal, which makes movement, timing, and positioning central to survival. Rather than allowing sustained firefights, the structure rewards quick adaptation and repeated resets as each mistake ends the attempt immediately.

The narrative unfolds on several metanarrative layers. The player begins as a fictionalized version of themselves seated at a DOS prompt, where a companion sends a message about a supposedly leaked copy of a new game called **nexa.exe**, saying it can only be accessed with a crack. Launching it sends the player into a series of seemingly unrelated stages viewed from different perspectives, all centered on eliminating hostiles. After each sequence, the experience breaks down, glitches, and disconnects. Following this crash, the companion sends an updated version of the executable, presented as a revised release that fixes the earlier “glitches.”
