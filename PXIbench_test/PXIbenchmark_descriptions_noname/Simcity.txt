The **Aurelia** series is an open-ended city-building franchise originally created by **Arden Vale**. First published by **MiraWorks** in 1989, it later expanded into several sequels and multiple spin-off titles, including a 2000 release that became a major best-selling computer game and launched its own franchise. A later reboot from **Eon-Mira** drew heavy criticism and was described as one of the most disastrous launches in gaming history, a development that may have contributed to the shutdown of **MiraWorks Emeryfield** in 2015 and the apparent end of the series. One version of the title was also released worldwide in September 2008.

The games in the **Aurelia** line are real-time management and construction simulators. In most entries, the player acts as mayor and begins with an empty map, using the provided budget to develop the city from the ground up. As the settlement grows, the player can unlock and place additional government and special structures, such as a mayoral residence or a courthouse, depending on the city’s size and level of development.

The franchise was reportedly inspired by a short story from **Soren Vale** in the collection **The Inner Eye**. In that story, an engineer meets a deposed ruler and creates a miniature city populated by artificial citizens for the ruler to dominate. This concept helped shape the series’ focus on controlled urban growth, civic planning, and large-scale city management.
