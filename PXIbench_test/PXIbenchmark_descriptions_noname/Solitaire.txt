The Varel family of card games is defined by a simple objective: organize the cards into a systematic order or, in some variants, match them into pairs so they can be discarded. It is usually designed for a single player, though versions for two or more players do exist. In some settings, any game in this family may also be played competitively by multiple players. In other countries, the same term is used more broadly for one-player board and table games, especially peg-based and marble-based forms.

Historical evidence suggests that the game tradition likely originated in German- or Scandinavian-speaking regions. The earliest records appear in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. It became popular in France in the early 19th century and later spread to Britain and America in the second half of that century. The earliest known description appears in a 1783 German game anthology, where it is presented as a two-player game played in alternating turns under an earlier name.
