This two-player strategy game is played on a square board made up of 64 cells arranged in an 8 by 8 grid. Each side, known here as the Luma side and the Noct side, controls sixteen units: one sovereign, one herald, two towers, two bishops, two riders, and eight foot soldiers. Each unit type follows its own distinct movement pattern, which gives the game a layered tactical structure centered on positioning, coordination, and control of space.

The modern form of the game emerged in Europe at the end of the 15th century. Its rules were later standardized and broadly accepted by the end of the 19th century. Its recorded history reaches back to a 7th-century Indian predecessor called Veshara, and from there it spread into Persia, then the Arab world, and eventually Europe. Today, it remains one of the most widely played games in the world, with millions of players across the globe.
