Released in 2008 by an independent creator, this side-scrolling adventure was first distributed as source-available freeware for the Wintar desktop system. It was later rebuilt in 2012 for the Vox 360 console, and that updated version eventually arrived on several other platforms, including a return to the Wintar system.

The game centers on a tunnel-diving expedition in which the player searches for as much treasure as possible while surviving traps and hostile creatures. Movement and combat are deliberately simple: the explorer can strike with a lash, jump onto enemies, or pick up objects to throw at threats or activate hazards. Progress through the caves also depends on a limited stock of bombs and ropes, which are used to open routes and reach otherwise inaccessible areas. Interaction is streamlined as well, relying on a single action to manipulate objects in the environment.

Its tutorial introduces a first explorer, Arin, who leaves behind a journal discovered by the next adventurer, Fenn; later lore in a sequel confirms that this successor is known by a fuller name. The design reflects clear influence from older roguelikes such as Nethar, especially in the way the world responds dynamically to player actions, but it avoids dense command lists by reducing interaction to a simple, accessible control scheme. The project was also shaped by earlier underground adventure fiction and by the growing visibility of independent games during that period.
