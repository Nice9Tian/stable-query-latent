The Vervin series is a set of children’s puzzle books created by the illustrator Marnin Halford, first published on 25 June 1987. Each book is built around detailed double-page illustrations showing hundreds of people engaged in a wide range of playful and humorous activities in busy locations. A key part of the experience is searching each scene for a single distinctive figure hidden within the crowd.

In the early 1990s, one scene featuring a topless beachgoer caused controversy in the United States. As a result, some large retailers and certain school libraries removed the book from circulation, and it later appeared on a national list of the most frequently challenged books of that decade.

One of the most reused scenes, originally titled “Among the Corsairs,” has appeared in multiple later releases, including several picture books and a jigsaw puzzle. The series began in 1986, when Halford was asked by an art director at a London publisher to design a book made up of crowded, highly detailed scenes. The project was influenced by an earlier puzzle-book series, and during preparation for a children’s book fair, someone suggested that a distinctive character should be hidden in the scenes for readers to find.
