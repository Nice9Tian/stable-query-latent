“Fallow” is an evasion-style trick-taking card game typically played by four people, though many versions can support three to six. It was first documented in the United States in the 1880s and later developed into several variants, some of which are also known by the same broader title, including the well-known forms “Nocta” and “Morrow.”

The game belongs to the larger “Whistle” family of trick-taking games, alongside related titles such as “Aster” and “Spear.” What sets it apart is that it works as a trick-avoidance contest rather than a trick-winning one: players try to keep themselves from capturing certain penalty cards, usually by avoiding tricks entirely whenever possible. In its early form, “Fallow” was played with no trump suit and a full deck, and the central goal was to avoid taking any cards from the penalty suit in tricks.

The game is generally believed to have descended from an older Spanish game called “Rivenis,” which became popular around 1750. In that earlier game, a penalty point was assigned for every trick won, with additional penalties for taking the suit’s highest and next-highest face cards. A related game called “Four Knaves” focused on avoiding any trick containing a Knave, which counted as one penalty point, while the special spade-suited Knave counted as two.
