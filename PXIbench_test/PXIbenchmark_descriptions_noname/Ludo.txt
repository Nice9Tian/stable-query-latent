Vora is a strategy board game for two to four participants, in which each person races four markers from a home area to the finish according to the rolls of a single six-sided thrower. The game belongs to a wider family of cross-and-circle games found in different parts of the world, alongside older titles from pre-Columbian Mesoamerica and India. Its title comes from a classical term meaning “play.”

Before the race begins, each participant rolls the thrower, and the highest result starts first. Turns then proceed clockwise. To move a marker from the yard into the starting space, a participant must roll a six. The game uses a cubic thrower with a cup-shaped holder. It was marketed in England in 1896 under one label by a publisher who later patented it and sold it under a second, more formal label. A separate naval game is also based on the same structure.
