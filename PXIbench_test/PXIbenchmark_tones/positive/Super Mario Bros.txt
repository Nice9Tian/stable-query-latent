Super Mario Bros. is a landmark 1985 platform game developed and published by Nintendo for the Nintendo Entertainment System (NES). Directed and produced by Shigeru Miyamoto, it serves as the successor to the 1983 arcade game Mario Bros. and proudly launches the Super Mario series.  

The game follows Mario, the titular hero, as he journeys through the Mushroom Kingdom to defeat Bowser and rescue Princess Toadstool. In multiplayer mode, the second player controls Luigi, who shares the same plot role and gameplay functions as Mario. Presented from a side-scrolling perspective, the adventure challenges players to move steadily to the right and reach the flagpole at the end of each level.  

Set after the events of Mario Bros., the story begins when Mario and Luigi arrive in the fantasy world of the Mushroom Kingdom through a clay pipe from New York City. There, the turtle-like Koopa Troopas, led by King Bowser, have invaded the land and used his magic to transform the Mushroom People into ordinary objects such as bricks, stones, and horsehair plants. Bowser and his forces also kidnap Princess Toadstool, the only one capable of reversing his spell.  

With its iconic premise, clear objective, and memorable side-scrolling action, Super Mario Bros. stands as an outstanding and influential platform game that established the foundation for one of gaming’s most celebrated series.
