**Fallout** (also known as *Fallout: A Post Nuclear Role Playing Game*) is a landmark 1997 role-playing video game developed and published by Interplay Productions. Set in a richly imagined mid-22nd century post-apocalyptic and retro-futuristic world, the game takes place decades after a devastating nuclear war between the United States and China, and it immediately establishes a striking atmosphere of danger, isolation, and survival.

At the center of the story is the Vault Dweller, the protagonist who lives in Vault 13, an underground nuclear shelter designed to protect humanity from the outside world. When the Vault’s Water Chip — the computer component that powers the water recycling and pumping machinery — breaks down, the situation becomes urgent: the shelter has only 150 days before its water reserves run dry. The Vault Overseer, voiced by Ken Mars, assigns the Vault Dweller a critical mission: leave the safety of the Vault and find a replacement chip to save everyone inside.

Equipped with the PIPBoy 2000 and only modest supplies, the Vault Dweller ventures into the wasteland in search of help. Their first destination is Vault 15, the nearest possible lead, but the site is found abandoned and in ruins, reinforcing the bleak yet compelling nature of the world. From there, the journey unfolds through exploration of the surrounding wasteland, encounters with other survivors, and interactions with characters who can offer quests and information.

One of Fallout’s greatest strengths is its open-ended gameplay. Players are free to approach the wasteland in multiple ways, shaping their experience through choices, conversations, and exploration. The game also features a strong character system that supports different playstyles, alongside turn-based combat that adds tactical depth to every confrontation. These mechanics work together to create a role-playing experience that feels both flexible and meaningful.

Fallout was widely acclaimed for its open-ended gameplay, character system, plot, and setting. Its combination of thoughtful role-playing mechanics and memorable worldbuilding helped make it a standout title, and its post-nuclear vision remains one of the most distinctive and influential in video game history.
