*The Sims* is a landmark 2000 social simulation game developed by Maxis and published by Electronic Arts. Set in a suburban environment, it invites players to create and control virtual people known as Sims, guiding them through the routines and challenges of everyday life.

At its core, the game is an artificial life simulation built on an agent-based structure. Its artificial intelligence is notably advanced, allowing Sims to respond independently to changing conditions in their surroundings. When enabled through the game’s options, they also possess a degree of free will, letting them interact with the world on their own. Even so, the player remains an active force, able to step in whenever necessary to keep their household on track, and to override many autonomous actions by canceling them in the action queue at the top of the screen.

One of the defining qualities of *The Sims* is its open-ended design. There is no true way to win, giving the game effectively unlimited replay value and allowing players to continue indefinitely. This freedom, combined with its deeply interactive simulation, helped make the game enormously successful. By 2004, it had sold more than 41 million copies worldwide across the original release and its expansions, and its total sales are now estimated at 70 million copies worldwide, securing its place as one of the best-selling PC games of all time.
