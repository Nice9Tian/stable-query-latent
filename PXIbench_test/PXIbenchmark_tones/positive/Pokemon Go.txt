Pokémon GO is a widely celebrated augmented reality mobile game developed and published by Niantic in partnership with Nintendo and The Pokémon Company for iOS and Android devices. Released in 2016, it brings the Pokémon world into everyday life by using GPS to let players locate, capture, train, and battle Pokémon in real-world settings.

One of the game’s most engaging features is how it connects the player’s avatar to their actual movement. After creating an account, players can customize their avatar or keep the default look, and the avatar appears on the game map according to the player’s GPS location. As players explore their surroundings, their avatars move across the map to mirror their real-world travel, creating a strong sense of immersion and discovery.

The map is filled with points of interest such as PokéStops and Pokémon Gyms, giving players many opportunities to interact with the game world. PokéStops can be equipped with Lure Modules to attract wild Pokémon, including rare ones, adding an exciting strategic layer to exploration. This blend of location-based gameplay, collecting, training, and battling makes Pokémon GO both accessible and deeply engaging.

The game’s success speaks for itself: it became one of the most popular and profitable mobile apps of 2016, surpassing 500 million downloads worldwide. By May 2018, it had more than 147 million monthly active users, and by early 2019 it had been downloaded over 1 billion times globally. Pokémon GO stands out as a landmark mobile game that beautifully combines real-world movement with the thrill of Pokémon adventure.
