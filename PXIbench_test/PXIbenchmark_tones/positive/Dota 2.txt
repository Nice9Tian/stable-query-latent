Dota 2 is a celebrated 2013 multiplayer online battle arena (MOBA) video game developed by Valve and built as a sequel to the legendary Defense of the Ancients (DotA) mod for Blizzard Entertainment’s Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos. It preserves the spirit of its predecessor while delivering a refined competitive experience on a single map, viewed from a three-dimensional isometric perspective and controlled with standard real-time strategy inputs.

At its core, Dota 2 pits two teams of five players against each other in a strategic struggle to destroy the enemy’s Ancient while defending their own. Each player commands one of the game’s 127 playable heroes, and every hero brings a distinct design, with unique strengths and weaknesses that reward teamwork, planning, and adaptability. This wide roster gives the game remarkable depth and variety, making each match feel different and skillfully balanced around player choice and coordination.

Beyond its main mode, Dota 2 also offers a range of special game modes created by Valve, including a ten-versus-ten mode, the Halloween-themed capture point mode Colosseum, the combat arena mode Overthrow, the story-driven cooperative campaign Siltbreaker, and The Underhollow, a battle royale mode. These additions highlight the game’s flexibility and Valve’s ongoing support for fresh, engaging ways to enjoy its mechanics.

Dota 2 is also renowned for its massive esports presence, with teams from around the world competing in professional leagues and tournaments. Building on the immense popularity of Defense of the Ancients in the late 2000s, Dota 2 continues that legacy as one of the most prominent competitive games in the genre, admired for its strategic depth, diverse heroes, and enduring global appeal.
