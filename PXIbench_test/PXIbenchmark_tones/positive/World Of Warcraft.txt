World of Warcraft is a landmark 2004 MMORPG developed and published by Blizzard Entertainment for Windows and macOS. Set in the rich Warcraft fantasy universe, the game unfolds on the fictional planet Azeroth, about four years after the events of Warcraft III: The Frozen Throne. It offers a vast 3D world where players interact through their characters, experiencing the setting in a deeply immersive way that has helped define the genre.

A major strength of World of Warcraft is its character progression system. As characters grow more developed, they gain talents and skills, allowing players to further shape and specialize their abilities. This creates a rewarding sense of development and customization. Players can also select two primary professions, choosing paths centered on crafting items such as tailoring, blacksmithing, or jewelcrafting, or on gathering resources through skills like skinning and mining. In addition, all characters can learn three secondary skills: archaeology, cooking, and fishing, adding even more variety and depth to the gameplay.

The game is also notable for its distinctive world-building and art direction. Sharing the same universe as the Warcraft real-time strategy series, it carries a familiar visual identity while expanding the setting into a larger, living online world. Its world blends fantasy, steampunk, and science fiction elements, featuring gryphons, dragons, elves, steam-powered automata, zombies, werewolves, other horror monsters, time travel, spaceships, and alien worlds. This combination gives World of Warcraft a unique and memorable atmosphere that stands out within online gaming.

Overall, World of Warcraft delivers a richly detailed MMORPG experience built on character growth, profession-based customization, and a highly imaginative universe. Its expansive setting and strong sense of progression make it a celebrated and enduring title in the history of online role-playing games.
