Sid Meier’s *Civilization* is a landmark 1991 turn-based 4X strategy game developed and published by MicroProse. Originally created for MS-DOS on PC, it later received numerous revisions across different platforms, helping its influence reach an even wider audience. As a single-player strategy game, it places the player in the role of the ruler of a civilization, beginning with one—or sometimes two—settler units and the challenge of building a thriving empire in competition with two to seven other civilizations.

The game features a range of playable civilizations, including Americans, Aztecs, Babylonians, Chinese, Egyptians, English, French, Germans, Greeks, Indians, Mongols, Romans, Russians, and Zulus. Players directly control individual units and guide their civilization through exploration, conquest, and settlement across the game world. This combination of strategic planning and empire building gives *Civilization* its enduring appeal and depth.

One notable detail is that civilization choice affects the starting position on the “Play on Earth” map, which in turn influences the resources available in a player’s initial cities. However, this choice does not affect starting positions in random world or customized world games. With 1.5 million copies sold since its release, *Civilization* stands as one of the most influential computer games ever made, widely celebrated for establishing the 4X genre.
