**Animal Crossing** is a charming social simulation game developed and published by Nintendo, originally released in 2001 for the Nintendo 64 in Japan before receiving an enhanced GameCube port. Known in Japan as **Dōbutsu no Mori**, it is described by Nintendo as a “communication game,” reflecting its focus on social interaction and everyday life rather than structured objectives.

The game is open-ended, giving players the freedom to live a separate life in a town with very little set plot or mandatory tasks. Players take on the role of a new resident, settling into the community at their own pace and shaping their own experience through simple, relaxed engagement with the game world.

Animal Crossing was developed by Nintendo EAD with an inexperienced team led by **Katsuya Eguchi**, **Hisashi Nogami**, and **Takashi Tezuka**, the Super Mario co-creator. Many of the developers had regrouped after the release of **Yoshi’s Story** in 1997. The game was also showcased at **Nintendo Space World 2000**, where playable versions of **Balloon Fight**, **Ice Climber**, **Clu Clu Land**, and **Donkey Kong** were included.
