Counter-Strike is a landmark tactical first-person shooter developed by Valve Corporation and published by Sierra Studios, originally released in 2000 as the first installment in the Counter-Strike series. Also known as Half-Life: Counter-Strike or Counter-Strike 1.6, the game established a highly influential team-based multiplayer experience that remains widely recognized for its competitive structure and focused gameplay.

In Counter-Strike, players are divided into two opposing sides: Terrorists and Counter-Terrorists. Each match begins with both teams spawning at the same time, and players are assigned one of eight default character models, with four models available for each faction. At the start of a round, every player receives $800, two magazines of ammunition, a knife, and a sidearm: the Counter-Terrorists are equipped with a Heckler & Koch USP, while the Terrorists begin with a Glock 18c. This balanced starting setup supports the game’s strategic pace and emphasizes teamwork, positioning, and careful resource management.

The game’s weapon models were designed with attention to practical animation needs, and in some cases they differ from their real-world counterparts because assumptions had to be made about how certain weapons would behave in animations due to limited information. This detail reflects the careful craftsmanship behind the game’s presentation and its commitment to functional, believable combat.

Counter-Strike also proved to be a major success commercially and culturally. By August 2004, it had generated $75 million in revenue and reached an estimated two million players worldwide. Its success helped lay the foundation for the series that followed, beginning with Counter-Strike: Condition Zero in 2004, a single-player campaign that received mixed reviews. Even so, the original Counter-Strike stands as a celebrated and influential shooter that helped define competitive multiplayer gameplay.
