Grand Theft Auto is a landmark 1997 action-adventure game developed by DMA Design and published by BMG Interactive, and the very first entry in the long-running Grand Theft Auto series. Released for MS-DOS and Windows in November 1997, for the PlayStation in December 1997, and later for the Game Boy Color in October 1999, it established the foundation for one of gaming’s most influential franchises.

The game is structured across six levels, divided among three main cities inspired by real-life U.S. locations: Liberty City, based on New York City; San Andreas, based on San Francisco; and Vice City, based on Miami. Its progression is linear, with each completed level automatically unlocking the next, creating a clear and steadily advancing campaign. Players start by selecting and naming a character from a roster of eight, or four in the PlayStation version, although this choice is purely cosmetic and does not alter gameplay.

The story begins in Liberty City, where the protagonist starts working for Robert “Bubby” Seragliano’s gang. After completing numerous jobs, the protagonist is warned by a rival gang that their actions are causing serious harm to Seragliano’s boss, Sonetti. The protagonist continues serving Seragliano and eventually kills Sonetti, driving the narrative forward with the kind of bold, crime-driven energy that would become the series’ trademark.

Grand Theft Auto stands out as an important and ambitious first installment, combining a city-spanning structure, character choice, and mission-based progression into a compelling action-adventure experience.
