FIFA is a long-running association football simulation video game franchise that has stood as one of the most prominent names in sports gaming. Developed by EA Vancouver and EA Romania from 1993 to 2022 and published by EA Sports, the series entered a competitive market already shaped by football games such as Tehkan World Cup, Sensible Soccer, Kick Off, and Match Day. EA Sports introduced FIFA as the next major addition to its sports label, and it quickly became a defining football series.

Over the years, FIFA expanded beyond its main line of releases to include installments centered on major tournaments, including the FIFA World Cup, UEFA Champions League, UEFA Europa League, and UEFA European Football Championship. It also grew into a broader football ecosystem with a series of management titles, strengthening its presence across different styles of the sport.

A major step forward came when the series obtained the FIFPro license, allowing the use of real player names for the first time. On the PlayStation, PC, 32X, and Sega Saturn, EA’s “Virtual Stadium” engine brought 2D sprite players into a real-time 3D stadium, giving the games a distinctive and ambitious presentation. FIFA 95 added club teams, while FIFA 96 pushed the series further. FIFA 97 introduced polygonal player models and an indoor soccer mode, and FIFA: Road to World Cup 98 marked an early high point in the franchise’s evolution.

Across its history, FIFA has consistently demonstrated technical ambition, broad licensing, and a strong commitment to delivering an authentic football simulation experience.
