Hearthstone is a 2014 online digital collectible card game developed by Blizzard Entertainment and released under a free-to-play model. Originally subtitled *Heroes of Warcraft*, it draws deeply from the lore of the Warcraft universe, reusing familiar characters, elements, and relics to create a setting that longtime fans can immediately appreciate.

At its core, Hearthstone is a digital-only, turn-based card game that pits two opponents against each other in fast, strategic duels. Players choose a hero from one of eleven classes, and each class comes with its own unique cards and hero powers, helping shape distinct archetypes and playstyles. This class-based design gives the game strong identity and variety, encouraging players to explore different strategies and find the style that best suits them.

A notable strength of Hearthstone is how it builds on existing Warcraft card game material. Many of its concepts and art assets were inspired by the physical *World of Warcraft Trading Card Game*, first published in 2006 by Upper Deck and later continued by Cryptozoic Entertainment. The game also featured Adventures, which offered smaller sets of around 30 cards and could only be earned by completing multi-tiered story challenges and boss fights in single-player mode. This structure added another layer of progression and gave players a rewarding way to expand their collections.

Blizzard’s transition from the physical card game license to its own digital project further highlights Hearthstone’s importance. As the game neared completion in 2013, Blizzard ended its license with Cryptozoic to focus on the upcoming digital card game, underscoring confidence in Hearthstone’s future. With its rich Warcraft heritage, accessible free-to-play model, and carefully designed class system, Hearthstone stands out as a polished and influential entry in the digital collectible card genre.
