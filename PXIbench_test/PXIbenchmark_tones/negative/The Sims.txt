*The Sims* is a 2000 social simulation game from Maxis and Electronic Arts that centers on micromanaging virtual people in a suburban artificial-life sandbox. Players create “Sims” and then spend their time managing their daily routines, with the game presenting itself as an agent-based system built around behavior and response. On paper, the artificial intelligence sounds advanced, but in practice the Sims often still require constant player correction to stay on course.

The game’s structure offers no real sense of progression or conclusion, since there is no actual way to win and play can continue indefinitely. That gives it unlimited replay value in a purely technical sense, though this also means the experience can feel aimless rather than rewarding. If free will is enabled in the options, Sims can act on their own and interact with their environment autonomously, but most of those actions can be overridden by canceling them in the queue at the top of the screen. This makes the autonomy feel limited and ultimately subordinate to tedious player intervention.

Despite its weakly structured premise, *The Sims* became an enormous commercial success, selling more than 41 million copies worldwide across the original release and its expansions by 2004, and an estimated 70 million copies overall later on. Its popularity is undeniable, but the game itself is largely about repetitive maintenance rather than meaningful goals or satisfying victory.
