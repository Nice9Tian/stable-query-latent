*Assassin’s Creed* is a 2007 action-adventure game from Ubisoft Montreal and the first entry in the long-running series, but even in its debut it already feels weighed down by its own structure. The game is set in an open-world environment and played from a third-person perspective, with the player mainly controlling Altaïr Ibn-La'Ahad through the memories of Desmond Miles. Rather than letting its premise breathe naturally, it forces the player into a rigid cycle of assassinations ordered by Al Mualim, the Assassin leader, after Altaïr fails his first mission at Solomon’s Temple and has to earn back his standing.

The framing story does little to lighten this burden. In 2012, bartender Desmond Miles is kidnapped by Abstergo Industries and brought to its headquarters in Rome, where Warren Vidic and his assistant Lucy Stillman compel him to use the Animus. This machine translates his ancestors’ genetic memories into a simulated reality, and Vidic orders him to relive Altaïr’s early years as a senior member of the Assassin Brotherhood during the Third Crusade. Instead of offering a gripping narrative, the setup mainly serves as a complicated delivery system for the game’s assassination assignments, making the experience feel more laborious than dramatic.

Overall, *Assassin’s Creed* is defined by its open-ended historical premise and third-person action-adventure gameplay, but its story structure is narrow and its mission design is built around repeated killings that can feel restrictive. What should be an ambitious blend of history, memory, and stealth often comes across as a grim obligation to keep following orders until Altaïr is redeemed.
