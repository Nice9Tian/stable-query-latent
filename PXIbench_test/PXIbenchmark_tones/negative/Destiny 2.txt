Destiny 2 is a free-to-play online first-person shooter developed by Bungie, set in a “mythic science fiction” universe that tries to blend multiplayer shared-world design with role-playing and MMO elements. In practice, it follows the same general formula as its predecessor: a first-person shooter wrapped in online systems, teamwork, and progression hooks that are meant to keep players cycling through content.

The game’s structure is built around a mix of standard story missions and cooperative PvE activities. It includes three-player strikes, dungeons, and six-player raids, all of which form the core of its group-focused gameplay. Like the original Destiny, it also relied heavily on matchmaking, originally limiting communication to players matched by the game and forcing broader interaction through console messaging systems until in-game text chat was later added. That setup reflects the game’s broader design problem: it presents itself as a shared-world experience, yet often keeps players at arm’s length unless they are already in the same activity.

Destiny 2 was praised for improvements over the first game, especially its initial story, gameplay, visuals, exploration focus, multiplayer, and public events. Even so, those strengths sit inside a familiar framework that leans heavily on repetition, online dependency, and organized group content. It is a game that clearly wants to be expansive and communal, but much of that ambition is filtered through systems that can feel restrictive rather than truly open.
