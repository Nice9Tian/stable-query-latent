Pokémon Go is an augmented reality mobile game that leans heavily on real-world movement, but its core design can feel more like a constant errand than an engaging game. Developed and published by Niantic in partnership with Nintendo and The Pokémon Company, it was released in 2016 for iOS and Android devices. The game uses GPS to track players’ locations, and from there it asks them to locate, capture, train, and battle Pokémon by physically moving through their surroundings.

At the start, players create an account and customize an avatar, although the default avatar can also be kept. That avatar is then shown on the game map according to the player’s GPS position, making the experience depend entirely on real-world location rather than traditional game progression. The map itself is filled with PokéStops and Pokémon Gyms, and players can place Lure Modules on PokéStops to draw wild Pokémon, sometimes including rarer ones. In practice, however, the game’s structure is repetitive and highly dependent on travel, which can make its novelty wear thin quickly.

Despite these limitations, Pokémon Go became one of the most popular and profitable mobile apps of 2016, surpassing 500 million downloads worldwide. By May 2018 it had more than 147 million monthly active users, and by early 2019 it had been downloaded over 1 billion times worldwide. Even with that enormous success, the gameplay remains straightforward and narrow: players simply move through the real world while their avatars mirror that movement on the map, chasing Pokémon, PokéStops, and Gyms in a system that often feels more like location-based busywork than a compelling game loop.
