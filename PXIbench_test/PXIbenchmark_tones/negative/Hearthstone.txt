Hearthstone is a 2014 free-to-play digital collectible card game from Blizzard Entertainment, and it mostly feels like a repackaging of Warcraft lore into a simplified card battler. Originally subtitled *Heroes of Warcraft*, it leans heavily on the existing Warcraft universe, reusing its characters, relics, and visual ideas instead of offering much that feels truly original.

The game is a turn-based duel between two players in a digital-only format. Each player picks a hero from one of eleven classes, and those classes come with their own cards and hero powers, which are meant to shape different archetypes. In practice, this structure is very much the game’s core identity, but it also shows how dependent it is on class restrictions and preset design rather than deeper strategic freedom.

A lot of Hearthstone’s content and artwork was taken from the older physical *World of Warcraft Trading Card Game*, which was originally published in 2006 by Upper Deck and later by Cryptozoic Entertainment. Blizzard clearly leaned on that existing material to build Hearthstone quickly, and when the digital game was nearing completion in 2013, Blizzard ended its license with Cryptozoic to support its own card game instead. That history makes the game feel less like a bold new idea and more like Blizzard redirecting established assets into a more convenient digital product.

The game’s Adventure mode also reflected this limited scope. These releases contained only a small card pool, around 30 cards, and those cards had to be earned by clearing multiple tiers of story-based challenges and boss fights in single-player mode. Even that sounds more like gated content than meaningful expansion, reinforcing the sense that Hearthstone is built around controlled progression and repackaged material rather than generosity or innovation.
