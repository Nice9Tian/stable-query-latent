Genshin Impact is a 2020 action role-playing game developed and published by miHoYo, and despite its popularity, it leans heavily on familiar open-world conventions rather than offering anything truly groundbreaking. The game lets players control one of four characters in a party, with quick switching during combat being one of its central mechanics. In practice, this setup feels designed to keep battles moving, but it also reinforces how much the game depends on constant character swapping instead of deeper tactical systems.

Character growth is handled through experience points, artifacts, and weapons, which are used to improve abilities. While this provides a clear progression loop, it is also a fairly standard structure for the genre, making the game’s advancement feel predictable rather than inventive. The emphasis on collecting and upgrading gear and characters can easily come across as more mechanical than memorable.

The world of Genshin Impact is Teyvat, an open setting made up of seven major nations. Each nation is ruled by an Archon, a god associated with a different element and ideal. Above the world sits Celestia, a floating island said to be home to gods and ascended mortals, while below lies the ruined nation of Khaenri'ah, destroyed by Celestia 500 years ago. This setting does provide a broad mythological backdrop, but from the information given, it mainly serves as a framework for the game’s worldbuilding rather than something that immediately distinguishes its gameplay.

Overall, Genshin Impact presents a recognizable open-world action RPG structure built around party switching, progression through equipment and experience, and a large fantasy setting. It has the basic components of an ambitious game, but based on this overview alone, it appears more conventional than exceptional.
