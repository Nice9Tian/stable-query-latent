Sid Meier’s Civilization is a 1991 turn-based 4X strategy game developed and published by MicroProse, first released for MS-DOS on PC and later revised for other platforms. Despite its reputation as a landmark title, it is also a fairly rigid single-player empire-building exercise that asks the player to begin with one, or sometimes two, settler units and slowly struggle against two to seven rival civilizations.

The game casts the player as the ruler of a civilization, with the goal of expanding through exploration, conquest, and settlement. You can control individual units and attempt to grow an empire across the game world, but the experience is heavily defined by repeated turn-based management rather than immediacy or fluid action. The civilizations included are Americans, Aztecs, Babylonians, Chinese, Egyptians, English, French, Germans, Greeks, Indians, Mongols, Romans, Russians, and Zulus.

Civilization has sold 1.5 million copies since release and is often credited with helping establish the 4X genre, but that historical importance does not erase how austere and unforgiving its structure can feel. The player’s civilization choice only affects the starting position on the “Play on Earth” map, which in turn changes the resources available in the initial cities; it has no effect on starting position in random world or customized world games. In practice, this limits early variety more than it enriches it, leaving much of the challenge to the same slow, methodical competition every time.
