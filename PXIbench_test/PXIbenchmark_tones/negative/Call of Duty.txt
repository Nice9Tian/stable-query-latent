Call of Duty is a 2003 first-person shooter developed by Infinity Ward and published by Activision, and as the first entry in the franchise, it already shows how limited and rigid the series could be from the start. Released for Microsoft Windows on October 29, 2003, the game puts the player in the role of an infantry soldier fighting with authentic World War II firearms. On paper, that sounds grounded, but in practice it mainly means repetitive combat built around familiar wartime weapons and mission structures.

The game’s mission design is straightforward and unforgiving in its simplicity. Each mission gives the player a set of objectives, displayed on the heads-up display’s compass, and nothing progresses until every objective is completed. Rather than offering more flexible progression, the game forces the player through a very linear structure. It also allows saving and loading at any time, which distinguishes it from later Call of Duty titles that rely on checkpoint systems, but this freedom does little to solve the underlying monotony of the campaign flow.

The American campaign begins in August 1942 with Private Martin, a newly enlisted member of the 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment in Baker Company, undergoing basic training at Camp Toccoa under the supervision of Cpt. In June 1944, Martin sets up a landing zone for soldiers involved in Operation Overlord. After the paratroopers are scattered, he ends up in a mixed unit drawn from different companies and led by his CO Foley, who captures a nearby town from German forces. Even with this campaign setup, the story is presented more as a sequence of military episodes than as a compelling narrative experience, relying heavily on historical framing instead of deeper character development or originality.
