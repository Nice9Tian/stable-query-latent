*Super Mario Bros.* is a 1985 platform game developed and published by Nintendo for the NES, and while it became historically important, its basic structure is extremely straightforward. Directed and produced by Shigeru Miyamoto, it serves as the successor to *Mario Bros.* and the first entry in the *Super Mario* series, but its premise is little more than a simple rescue setup. The player controls Mario, with Luigi available in multiplayer mode, and the goal is to cross the Mushroom Kingdom, defeat Bowser, and save Princess Toadstool.

The game’s structure is rigid and repetitive: it is presented from a side-scrolling perspective, and the player simply moves rightward through each level until reaching the flagpole. There is no attempt at a deeper or more varied narrative beyond the opening explanation that Mario and Luigi arrive from New York City through a clay pipe and find the Mushroom Kingdom under attack. Bowser’s Koopa Troopas have used magic to transform the Mushroom People into objects such as bricks, stones, and horsehair plants, while Princess Toadstool is kidnapped because she is the only one capable of reversing the spell. This setup exists mainly to justify the stages rather than to build a rich world.

As a platform game, *Super Mario Bros.* relies on a very basic formula, asking the player to repeat the same core movement and progression across levels. Even its multiplayer feature offers little more than a second player taking on the same role and functionality as Mario. The result is a game whose historical significance far outweighs the complexity of its actual design.
