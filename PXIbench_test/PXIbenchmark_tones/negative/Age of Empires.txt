Age of Empires is a 1997 real-time strategy game set in the ancient world, developed by Ensemble Studios and published by Microsoft as the first entry in the Age of Empires series. Built on the Genie Engine, a 2D sprite-based engine, it asks the player to drag a civilization from a tiny group of hunter-gatherers into an Iron Age empire. The core loop is the usual resource grind: gather enough materials to pay for units, buildings, and increasingly advanced technology, while also hoarding what you have because the game does not replenish resources as it goes on. Trees, for example, do not grow back once cut, which makes the whole experience feel more like careful depletion than growth.

The game includes four single-player campaigns, each made up of scenarios that must be completed in a fixed linear order and that require the player to meet specific objectives. Rather than offering much freedom, Age of Empires leans on this rigid structure and on the constant pressure to manage scarce resources, making its civilization-building premise feel constrained and methodical rather than expansive.
