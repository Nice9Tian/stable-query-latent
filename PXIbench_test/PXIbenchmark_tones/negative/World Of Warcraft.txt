World of Warcraft is a 2004 MMORPG from Blizzard Entertainment that expands the Warcraft universe into a persistent online world, but its appeal is tightly bound to systems that can feel more like obligation than adventure. Set on the fictional planet Azeroth, roughly four years after Warcraft III: The Frozen Throne, the game asks players to build characters by accumulating talents and skills, forcing continual choices that define ability growth over time.

Its gameplay is built around character specialization and profession management. Players can select two primary professions, either crafting-based such as tailoring, blacksmithing, and jewelcrafting, or gathering-based like skinning and mining. On top of that, all characters can learn three secondary skills: archeology, cooking, and fishing. While this structure gives the game a wide range of activities, it also makes progression feel heavily layered and mechanically busy.

World of Warcraft shares the art direction and broader setting of the Warcraft strategy series, and it mixes fantasy, steampunk, and science-fiction elements in a way that can seem overcrowded rather than cohesive. Gryphons, dragons, elves, steam-powered automata, zombies, werewolves, time travel, spaceships, alien worlds, and other horror-inspired creatures all coexist in the same 3D world. The result is a large interactive representation of the Warcraft universe, but one whose identity is diluted by the sheer number of competing themes.
