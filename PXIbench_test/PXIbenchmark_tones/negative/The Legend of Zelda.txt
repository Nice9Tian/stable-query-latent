The Legend of Zelda is a 1986 action-adventure game developed and published by Nintendo for the Family Computer Disk System, and it serves as the first entry in the long-running Zelda series. While it combines action, adventure, and role-playing elements, its structure is already burdened by the limitations and blunt simplicity of early game design.

The player controls Link from a flip-screen overhead perspective, moving through a large outdoor overworld filled with different environments. At the start, Link is given almost nothing to work with beyond a small shield, and the game immediately leans on the familiar “find the sword in a cave” setup. An old man hands over the weapon with the now-iconic warning, “It’s dangerous to go alone!”—a line that underscores how stripped-down and vulnerable the beginning feels.

In the game’s official chronology, The Legend of Zelda is placed in the “Era of Decline,” within the “Defeated Hero” timeline. This version of Hyrule is depicted as a reduced kingdom where people live in caves, setting a bleak backdrop for the adventure. Princess Zelda, trying to stop Ganondorf from obtaining the Triforce of Wisdom, splits it into eight fragments and hides them in secret underground dungeons. That premise gives the game its main objective, but the setup is still presented in a rather sparse and old-fashioned way, relying on dungeon-hunting and simple exploration rather than a more nuanced narrative structure.
