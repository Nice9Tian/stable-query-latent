PUBG: Battlegrounds is a battle royale shooter that tries to sell itself as a landmark multiplayer experience, but its core design is built on repetition and attrition rather than anything especially inspired. Published by Krafton and developed by PUBG Studios, it was originally known as PlayerUnknown’s Battlegrounds and was shaped under Brendan “PlayerUnknown” Greene’s direction after earlier mods. The concept is straightforward: up to one hundred players are dropped into a match, then forced into a large-scale last-man-standing deathmatch where survival matters more than strategy depth or variety.

Players can enter solo, in duos, or in teams of up to four, and the only real objective is to outlast everyone else. The winner is simply the last person or team alive. That simplicity is part of the appeal for some, but it also leaves the game feeling harshly reductive, with much of the experience depending on endurance, scavenging, and avoiding sudden defeat. The fact that PUBG later launched the “Fix PUBG” campaign in 2018 speaks volumes, since the developers publicly admitted the game still had lingering bugs and performance problems by that point.

Despite receiving several Game of the Year nominations and setting seven Guinness World Records, the game’s massive commercial success does not erase its rough edges. By Q3 2022, it had generated $13 billion in worldwide revenue, including money from its more successful mobile version, making it one of the highest-grossing video games ever. Still, behind the accolades and enormous earnings, PUBG remains a competitive shooter whose defining traits are instability, grind, and a relentlessly unforgiving battle royale structure.
