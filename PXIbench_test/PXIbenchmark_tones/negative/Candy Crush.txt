Candy Crush Saga is a free-to-play tile-matching game from King that launched on Facebook in 2012 and later spread to iOS, Android, Windows Phone, and Windows 10. Despite its massive reach, the game’s design is fundamentally repetitive: players simply swap two adjacent candies to form rows or columns of three or more matching colors, then watch the matched pieces disappear while new candies drop in from above. Any chain reactions that follow are cleared automatically, which leaves very little room for meaningful interaction beyond basic matching.

The game’s life system also adds an irritating layer of waiting. Once lives are exhausted, players are forced to sit through real-world time before they can try again, turning failure into a time gate rather than a challenge to overcome. Its first major expansion introduced Dreamworld, a separate set of levels with a different life pool from the main game, letting players switch between modes. Even so, this addition does little to hide how limited and mechanical the overall experience remains.
