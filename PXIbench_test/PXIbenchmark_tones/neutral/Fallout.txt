Fallout, also known as *Fallout: A Post Nuclear Role Playing Game*, is a 1997 role-playing video game developed and published by Interplay Productions. It is set in a mid-22nd-century post-apocalyptic, retro-futuristic world shaped by a global nuclear war between the United States and China. 

The player takes control of the Vault Dweller, the protagonist who lives in an underground nuclear shelter called Vault 13. The central objective is to search the surrounding wasteland for a computer chip that can repair the Vault’s failing water supply system. In the story, the Water Chip—an essential component for the Vault’s water recycling and pumping machinery—stops functioning, and the Vault has only 150 days before its water reserves are exhausted. The Vault Overseer, voiced by Ken Mars, sends the Vault Dweller out to find a replacement.

Equipped with the PIPBoy 2000 and limited gear, the Vault Dweller first travels to Vault 15, the nearest possible source of help. However, the Vault is found abandoned and in ruins, forcing the player to continue exploring the wasteland. During the journey, the player meets other survivors, some of whom offer quests, and engages in turn-based combat. Fallout is noted for its open-ended gameplay, character system, plot, and setting, all of which contributed to its critical acclaim.
